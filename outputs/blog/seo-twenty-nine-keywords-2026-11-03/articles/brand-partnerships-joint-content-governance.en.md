# Brand Partnerships: Govern Joint Content and Approvals

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

When two brands make content together, establish the shared proposition and each brand’s facts before aligning creators, reviewers and channels on one version.

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

Brand partnerships are collaborations between brands around a defined objective. This guide focuses on governing content made or distributed by two brands: which proposition they express together, which product facts each owns, which version creators receive, who can approve what and how changes travel. That differs from discussing only a transaction between one brand and an individual creator. A public joint announcement shows how a partnership was announced but usually cannot reconstruct its private agreement, internal responsibilities or incremental returns. The approach below separates a sourced example from editorial operating recommendations so joint content has traceable facts and accountable owners. It is a working framework, not a universal contract.

## Start with a shared objective before arranging two logos

Joint-brand content should first explain the shared problem, audience and reason both parties are needed. Two logos in one image establish a visual combination, not the collaboration’s value.

Draft a proposition for discussion, then identify the products, knowledge, situations and channels each party can contribute. A shared objective does not require identical language from both brands, but the messages should not contradict each other.

The [collaboration workflow guide](/blog/collaborate-with-influencers-brand-workflow/#answer) turns objectives into content assignments. Decide whether the work explains, demonstrates or invites participation before choosing a creator role, rather than start with fame and infer a campaign purpose afterwards.

Keep objective, hypothesis and observed result distinct. A plan to reach a new audience does not establish incremental audience growth; content emphasizing shared experience does not independently demonstrate a changed consumer perception. This distinction helps a project preserve an ambitious creative purpose while remaining accurate about what has actually been measured and what is still an intended outcome.

## What a public example establishes: Nike and LEGO

The [Nike and LEGO joint announcement](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-lego-partnership-official-experience-product-details) dated May 29, 2025 describes a multi-year partnership involving experiences, co-branded products and digital content. It supplies a bounded example of collaboration spanning several content and product touchpoints.

The announcement does not disclose the agreement, approval matrix, creator payments or incremental sales analysis. The recommendations below are not represented as that partnership’s actual internal process, and marketing language is not a performance guarantee.

A brand researcher can retain the announced purpose and touchpoints while separately listing unavailable information. Missing private records do not prevent a useful example, but they limit operational and financial conclusions.

Likewise, a historical announced release date does not confirm present inventory or purchase eligibility. Keep the announcement date instead of rewriting it as current commercial terms. This avoids turning an accessible public case into a source for details it never supplied, while still allowing the team to learn from the breadth of the publicly described collaboration.

## Assign owners for the shared proposition and separate facts

Create a fact list separating the shared campaign proposition, brand A product facts, brand B product facts and the creator’s own experience. Approval of layout does not automatically confer authority over another brand’s product statements.

Tie each material statement to evidence, applicable version and confirming owner. If a function belongs to only one product, a joint image should not imply that the other product has it too.

Obtain actual project confirmation for timing, geography, redemption and pricing conditions. This guide inserts no fictional commercial terms and supplies no universal legal determination; the assignment is to make each sentence traceable to its factual source.

Preserve unknowns and explain which production or publication decision they block. Do not make a brief look complete by transferring an unanswered brand question to the creator. A clear pending fact can be resolved by its owner, whereas an unsupported statement may become harder to correct after it has entered a script, recording and multiple channel versions.

## Give creators one master brief with explicit exceptions

The [creator-scope guide](/blog/creator-contract-scope-checklist/#answer) separates deliverables, channels, uses and changes. Two participating brands do not automatically mean twice the content, unlimited revisions or complete use by two separate accounts.

Provide one master brief and one day-to-day coordinator, with the necessary facts from each brand attached. Mark channel-specific exceptions individually instead of allowing two conflicting documents to remain final.

A creator can suggest natural wording but cannot repair unsupported product statements through a personal tone. Explain which rewrites are permitted and which information must return to the factual owner before recording begins.

When scope changes, determine whether deliverables and commercial arrangements need updating. Do not call added material a small tweak while workload and use expand without a decision. Clear exceptions preserve creative freedom inside an understood task and reduce the chance that a creator follows one brand’s instruction only to discover that the other brand expected a different asset entirely.

## Separate fact review, brand expression and commercial acceptance

An approval record can distinguish factual confirmation, brand expression, use scope, publication arrangements and final acceptance, with an appropriate owner for each. Not every participant has authority over every column.

The [Frame.io version-comparison guide](https://help.frame.io/en/articles/9952618-comparison-viewer) describes comparing versions and comments as a content-review example. An approval action inside a tool does not independently complete commercial acceptance or another party’s factual review.

Retain the reviewed version and unresolved comments. If the brands disagree, an identified project decision-maker should resolve the conflict rather than leave the creator to guess which comment carries more authority.

The [commercial-memo guide](/blog/brand-deals-commercial-memo-content-brief/#answer) further separates commercial agreement and creative assignment. Make approved refer to a specific matter rather than an objectless green check. This lets a later operator distinguish a script cleared for factual accuracy from a delivery accepted commercially, and avoids relying on a single status word to settle several different decisions that may still be pending.

Suppose one brand has cleared the image while the other is still checking a product function. Publication should remain pending rather than pass by majority vote. Different approvals answer different questions and cannot cancel each other out. The coordinator records the remaining question and next respondent, then distinguishes a decision to continue production from a decision to publish publicly. That preserves momentum where appropriate without describing an unfinished fact review as completed.

## Plan publication order and the path for changes

State first publishing channel, other channel versions, planned timing and actual owners. A shared plan does not establish simultaneous publication everywhere or readiness of every account’s tool permissions.

Suppose brand A corrects a product name. The master brief, subtitles, landing page and brand B’s repost wording may all be affected. This is an editorial scenario, not a client event. Its purpose is to identify dependencies rather than claim one file change completes the work.

Record what was updated, what remains and whether use should pause. A message saying the new version was sent is not evidence that every team replaced the old material.

A change identifier or clear date and version can suffice; a small project need not acquire a complex system. The standard is whether the next owner can identify the correct asset and where an old version may still appear. That practical traceability matters more than the number of status fields in a document.

## Handle brand-to-brand and creator relationships separately

A co-branding fact does not replace explanation of a creator’s actual commercial relationship. Payment, gifted products or another material connection should be addressed under the real arrangement and applicable geography.

The [FTC endorsement FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) supplies US-context guidance on relationships and truthful expression, not a worldwide disclosure template. No label here guarantees that every market, platform and expression is covered.

Both brands should also examine whether a creator’s public statement expands experience with one product into a claim about the entire collaboration range. Actual experience, filmed version and statement object should remain aligned.

If information is unconfirmed, route the question to an appropriate owner. An approaching publication date is not a reason to use the creator’s personal credibility to fill a gap in facts or relationships. These checks concern what the content communicates, alongside the separate operational question of whether each brand has approved its release.

## Joint results are not two scorecards simply added together

The [AMEC evaluation framework](https://amecorg.com/amecframework/) distinguishes outputs, audience response and outcomes. Joint content can retain channel delivery and business measures separately, but first establish overlap, differing definitions and attribution windows.

Do not add reach reports and describe the result as distinct people without deduplication. An order visible to both parties is not automatically two incremental sales. Where records are insufficient, retain separate results and explain why they cannot be combined.

The [campaign-measurement guide](/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) pairs objectives with observations. More comments on a joint release do not independently establish improved brand equity, consumer preference and profit for both brands.

A final report can describe what the collaboration did, what was observed and which explanations need research. It need not manufacture unmeasured benefits to present a win for both sides. Keeping measures separate where necessary is part of making the partnership understandable, not a failure to produce a single impressive campaign total.

## Hand off a joint-content record another team can use

Hand off proposition, fact list, master brief, approval scope, actual versions, publication state and change history. Both brand owners and creators should be able to locate the decisions relevant to their work.

The value of joint-content governance is preserving a sentence’s meaning as it moves among teams. It does not guarantee a successful partnership or replace actual commercial arrangements and appropriate specialist review.

QuickKOL can help interpret creator assignments and research relevant work. Each brand’s facts, permissions, terms and results still require verification. Match the shared expression to responsibilities before expanding the collaboration. Clear boundaries also make future review easier for both participating teams.

## Responsibility boundaries for joint content

| Object | Useful record | Does not establish |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Shared proposition | Mutually confirmed task and wording | Every benefit achieved |

| Product facts | Each brand’s evidence and owner | Layout approval equals fact confirmation |

| Creator delivery | Master brief, uses and exceptions | Unlimited use by both brands |

| Outcome report | Definitions, window, overlap and limits | Two totals can simply be added |

Editorial recommendations, not Nike and LEGO’s private process or agreement.

## Joint-content handoff checklist

Give each material decision an object and owner.

- [ ] Shared objective and hypothesis are explicit.

- [ ] Brand-specific facts have separate owners.

- [ ] Creators receive one master version.

- [ ] Approval identifies what was approved.

- [ ] Corrections reach affected locations.

- [ ] Results retain comparability and overlap limits.
