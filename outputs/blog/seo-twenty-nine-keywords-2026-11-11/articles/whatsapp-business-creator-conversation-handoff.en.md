# WhatsApp for Business in a Creator Campaign

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Give creator-led inquiries a consented destination, a responsible team and a usable campaign record.

![A creator and cafe owner coordinate a campaign at the counter](../public/assets/blog/whatsapp-business-creator-conversation-handoff.jpg)

WhatsApp for Business can receive inquiries generated by a creator campaign when the business has a clear destination, a staffed response process and appropriate recipient permission. Give the creator one accurate invitation, give the business ownership of product and customer questions, and give the campaign manager a record of what the conversation can establish. Rehearse the journey before publication. Measure link activity, qualified conversations and confirmed outcomes separately. A publicly visible phone number is not a substitute for permission to send promotional messages. The setup should make the creator’s recommendation easier to act on without turning their personal inbox into the brand’s customer service desk.

## Choose a campaign outcome that needs a conversation

Start with the question a prospective customer cannot resolve from the public post. A restaurant may need to discuss accessibility or a group reservation. A software tutorial may generate requests for a demonstration. A home product may require a conversation about dimensions or installation. If the answer is already a price and a straightforward checkout, adding a chat could create unnecessary delay. Define one question that benefits from a response, then decide whether the creator should promote that destination.

During sourcing, review whether a candidate actually explains similar questions in their work. Someone who demonstrates setup may suit a considered purchase better than someone whose main strength is visual discovery. Use the [creator brief](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/write-a-useful-campaign-brief/#answer) to record the audience question, business owner, service hours and intended next step. Let the creator express the invitation naturally. A useful selection criterion is whether the explanation helps people understand what to ask. The size of a following cannot establish that those conversations will be relevant.

## Identify the business and the recipient’s permission

The [WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/) requires accurate business identity and recipient permission. Make the destination recognizable as the actual merchant or service provider. The creator’s invitation should explain whose team will answer and what the conversation is for. Someone following a personal recommendation should be able to tell when they have entered the business’s support process.

Record the scope and method of permission in the campaign design. A question about opening hours does not establish consent to unrelated future promotions. Check the current policy, applicable law and actual product configuration before a follow-up sequence is activated. Test an opt-out using fictional customer information and confirm that both the agency and business stop the relevant messages. Keep only the evidence needed to demonstrate the preference. A shared campaign spreadsheet rarely needs the complete private conversation. One named owner should maintain the current contact preference when temporary campaign staff rotate off the project.

## Distinguish the Business App from the Platform workflow

[Meta’s business chat controls announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/ways-to-manage-your-businesses-chats-on-whatsapp/) emphasizes the recipient’s control over business messages. Implementation also depends on which product the business uses. The Business App and the Business Platform should not be treated as one interchangeable set of scheduling, automation and billing rules. For a Platform deployment, have the implementation owner verify the applicable service window and approved-template requirements in the current policy and configuration.

Ask for a rehearsal using the actual account. One person follows the creator’s invitation, another receives and assigns the question, and a third checks the out-of-hours behavior. A cafe handling a small reservation queue may need a different setup from a brand routing installation questions across teams. Record account ownership, operators, providers, charges and the failure contact. A demonstration of an automated greeting does not establish continuous human support or complete sales attribution. The campaign should promise the response process that staff and settings can deliver.

## Give the creator an invitation the business can fulfill

Treat the invitation as a deliverable in the brief. Specify what can be asked, who replies, the service hours and any non-sensitive information that would help. For example, a furniture creator could invite viewers to ask the brand whether a desk fits a measured workspace. Have the business confirm that staff can answer before the creator records it. Avoid promises about stock, discounts or response speed that nobody has approved.

Review the spoken invitation, captions, destination and business profile together. A changed number or service territory can invalidate an earlier approval. A paid or gifted relationship still needs the appropriate disclosure even when the commercial conversation takes place elsewhere. For U.S.-relevant promotions, the [FTC’s influencer disclosure guide](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf) provides a starting point for the material connection. The brand checks product facts and offer conditions. The creator checks their experience and voice. Customer-facing staff should prepare against the same approved version.

## Route inquiries to someone who can answer them

Define four practical queues for a pilot: partnership proposals, product questions, order support and contact preferences. Assign each to a person who can act. The creator can discuss their own experience, while the merchant handles refunds, order verification and customer-specific commitments. A clear handoff can be as simple as an approved destination and a short explanation of the issue. Requiring a creator to collect order identifiers creates extra handling without necessarily resolving anything.

The [FTC’s business information protection guide](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/pdf-0136_proteting-personal-information.pdf) recommends understanding information flows and retaining what the business needs. Apply that principle with an internal inquiry ID, a category, an owner and a status. Avoid distributing private chat screenshots to every campaign participant. Where identity verification is necessary, let authorized support perform it in the appropriate channel. Define escalation for product faults and payment problems. A difficult question may indicate a genuine customer need, so classify its substance before treating it as evidence of poor creator performance.

## Separate conversation origin from commercial outcomes

Give each creator a traceable route or an optional campaign prompt, then treat it as an origin clue. People may watch several creators, search independently or remove a prefilled message. Staff can ask a short optional source question and retain unknown origins. Track clicks, conversations, qualified inquiries, bookings and orders as separate observations. They are neither interchangeable events nor independent populations to be added together.

Suppose the business receives 40 relevant inquiries, 25 carrying a known creator-origin clue, 18 meeting its predefined qualification rule, and eight resulting in a booking. Those figures describe different steps with possible overlap. They do not establish that the creator generated 40 incremental customers. Agree deduplication and the observation period before comparing partners. When many people ask the same specification question, the next brief may need a clearer demonstration. The [creator campaign measurement framework](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) can help decide which observation supports an acceptance decision and which supports a future creative change.

## Use a bounded pilot to decide whether to expand

Limit the first pilot to one creator, one answerable invitation and defined service hours. Check the destination, permission process, opt-out and escalation before release. After publication, record inquiry categories, reasons for delay and unresolved handoffs. If the queue lacks capacity, change staffing or the release plan. If most questions concern missing public information, improve that information before buying more conversation volume.

The final handoff should contain the live creator URL, approved invitation, business destination, origin rule, anonymized question summary and owners of outstanding tasks. Give the creator feedback on the explanation and contracted delivery. Give the business feedback on response quality. Expansion becomes reasonable when the journey consistently serves relevant inquiries and records a verifiable next step. Write that condition into the next purchase scope so the creator, agency and merchant know who produces the content and who finishes the conversation.

## Rehearse capacity with a booking example

Suppose a cafe asks two creators to introduce small event bookings. The first publication goes live on Friday evening, while the team will answer on Saturday morning. State those hours in the invitation. An acknowledgement should confirm receipt rather than promise availability. If both creators direct people to one destination, the owner needs a shared capacity check and a workable origin record. Someone asking through two routes should follow the business’s booking deduplication rule rather than becoming two customers in the recap.

Rehearse a question staff cannot immediately answer, such as whether a group can change the menu. The operator should route it to a manager with authority rather than turn the creator’s personal description into a guarantee. The creator is responsible for accurately explaining the approved experience. If bookings pause, the publishing owner needs to update the invitation or destination promptly. Correctly delivered content can be assessed separately from that operational correction. Keep the decision and handling effort so the next campaign can estimate what the business is able to receive. This exercise tests the complete path under an ordinary capacity constraint.

## Accept the connection and the response process separately

If an outside provider configures a team inbox, identify who owns the account, who can export records and how access is handed back at the end. The demonstration should include reassignment after an operator leaves, not only successful receipt by the administrator. A working connection does not establish that location staff know how to answer campaign questions. Have the business team perform routing, reply, escalation and closure themselves, noting where each step fails.

Suppose support receives the message but cannot find the offer conditions described in the creator’s invitation. That is a knowledge handoff problem. Purchasing more creator publications would not repair it. If an origin label disappears in reporting, assign the correction to configuration or analysis rather than demanding an extra advertisement from the creator. Each gap needs an observation, an owner and a review time. Before expanding, confirm that business staff can operate the journey and the provider can support its approved scope without depending on one outside person forwarding messages. This separates technical delivery from the actual service the audience receives.

## Conversation handoff responsibilities

| Question | Owner | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product fit | Brand product team | Approved specifications and answer |
| Partnership proposal | Creator partnership lead | Brief and next step |
| Order support | Authorized support | Internal ticket status |
| Stop contact | Preference owner | Scope and completion status |

Assign actual authority and restrict private conversations to the people who need them.

## Pre-launch conversation rehearsal

Run an end-to-end test from the creator’s destination using fictional customer details.

- [ ] Confirm business identity, hours and question scope.
- [ ] Check how permission and opt-out are recorded.
- [ ] Route product, partnership and support questions to named owners.
- [ ] Define origin clues, qualification and outcomes separately.
