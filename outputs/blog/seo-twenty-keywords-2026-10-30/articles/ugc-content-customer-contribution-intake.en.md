# UGC Content: Build a Customer Contribution Intake

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Build an intake process for customer-contributed photos, videos and text. Check origin, experience, permitted uses and versions before adding assets to a brand library.

![ A blue camping mug, coral mailer, product-use photographs, paper sleeve and silver clip on a bright tabletop. ](../public/assets/blog/ugc-content-customer-contribution-intake.jpg)

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

UGC content generally means content created or contributed by users. Before a brand reuses a customer photograph, review or video, establish who contributed what, the original context and whether the intended use is appropriate. Record spontaneous customer sharing separately from commissioned UGC-style production. Public visibility, a brand tag or delivery of a file does not independently establish permission for every commercial use.

## Classify the relationship behind the contribution

A phone-shot video might come from a spontaneous customer contribution, an incentivized submission, an employee’s experience or professional production under a brief. Its visual style does not establish the commercial relationship. Record that relationship before describing paid production as an independent customer contribution.

The [OECD user-created content report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/participative-web-and-user-created-content_9789264037472-en.html), published in 2007, examines forms, value chains and challenges of user creation. It provides historical context, not proof that every contemporary paid UGC service represents the same form of participation. The categories here are an editorial intake method rather than a universal definition.

The distinction changes the next question. A spontaneous photograph needs a conversation about the brand’s intended use. Commissioned work needs the agreed delivery and rights. A recommendation involving a gift needs attention to how the relationship is expressed. If the immediate purpose is to understand customer language, organize necessary observations before downloading and publicly republishing the original material. Let the actual use determine the evidence to request. An intake process should help the team decide whether research, permission outreach or a production handoff is appropriate, instead of putting every asset into one apparently ready folder.

## Create a stable record of the original contribution

Give each asset an internal identifier and retain its original link or appropriate reference, contributor account, observation date, file version and receipt channel. A public post and a private submission are different. A customer-support attachment should not automatically enter an open marketing folder. The record needs a route back to its origin, not just a downloaded filename.

Separate the submitter, depicted people and possible rights holders. A customer may submit a photograph taken by a friend or a video containing someone else’s music. Confirming the sender’s identity does not resolve every visual or audio permission.

Keep untraceable files pending rather than guessing their origin to meet a schedule. Request only information that can change the decision, such as authorship, version and intended use. A product still life does not justify collecting unrelated private information. Assign access to the people responsible for intake and review, avoiding multiple uncontrolled copies of records that cannot be updated together. A stable identifier lets an editor connect a crop, permission reply and final placement without treating three differently named files as three unrelated contributions.

## Preserve the conditions and context of the experience

A customer saying that a product was convenient on one trip does not establish suitability for every travel setting. Retain relevant conditions instead of turning an individual experience into a universal performance claim. Shortened quotations, rewritten captions and replacement product shots can change meaning, so review needs to address more than appearance.

In the United States advertising context, the [FTC endorsement FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) calls for honest, nonmisleading endorsements and addresses disclosure of material connections. When customer material becomes advertising, consider the message that presentation communicates. A brand tag is not automatically an adequate disclosure; the actual context matters.

Keep the original expression, proposed extract and editorial reason together. If a necessary limitation cannot survive the format, choose a different contribution or decline the use. Editing should not transform a qualified opinion into unconditional praise. Willingness to contribute also does not authorize the brand to add an experience or outcome the person never described. The reviewer should be able to compare the selected version with the source and explain why a reasonable reader would still understand the intended experience and its conditions.

## Confirm scope against the actual intended use

Explain where the brand intends to use which version, and in what form. An organic brand post, email, product page and paid advertisement are not automatically interchangeable uses. Identify territory, duration, edits, translations and additional elements. A vague request to share cannot stand in for every later commercial placement.

The [Copyright Office permission guide](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/m10.pdf) explains finding the current rights holder when permission is needed and checking third-party material within a work. It also recognizes limitations and exceptions that require evaluation. This is not a claim that every use must follow one permission procedure; it is a reason to establish an appropriate basis for the intended use.

A [content licensing schedule](/blog/influencer-content-licensing-paid-media-schedule/#answer) connects asset, channel, version and expiry. Record agreed, pending and excluded uses separately. If permission covers organic social use only, retain that condition when the file reaches an advertising team. A later placement requires checking the original scope and obtaining further confirmation where appropriate. The operational goal is that the person receiving an asset can identify its permitted use without searching through informal messages or assuming that an approved thumbnail means every associated file is ready for any channel.

## Keep incentives separate from contribution quality

Describe product access, discounts or rewards before requesting a contribution. The editorial process should not quietly turn an incentive into a requirement for a positive opinion. Record what the person actually received and whether production or publication was required. A noncash arrangement can still involve a relevant relationship.

The [gifting disclosure guide](/blog/influencer-gifting-disclosure/#answer) helps teams examine recommendations involving free products. An intake sheet does not automatically decide whether a disclosure is sufficient or reject every incentivized contribution. It preserves the relationship so the people responsible for the market and channel can handle it appropriately.

An asset may be unsuitable because of format, rights or task fit, rather than because the customer was insufficiently positive. Explain the next step without promising selection or an unapproved reward. If material originally arrived as part of a support request, resolve that customer issue first. Marketing participation should not become an undisclosed condition of obtaining help. Keeping support and optional contribution processes clear also gives the editor a more accurate account of why the material exists and what the contributor understood when providing it.

## Review the edited version as a separate asset

Keep the parent asset identifier for crops, captions, voiceovers and translated outputs. Editors need to know which conditions must remain, which shots cannot be replaced and whether the proposed combination is within scope. Passing intake does not approve every subsequent edit.

The [asset version handoff guide](/blog/ugc-creator-editing-rights-version-matrix/#answer) helps arrange versions, ownership and expiry. Separate technical acceptance from content acceptance: a playable file does not establish an accurate quotation, while correctly spelled subtitles do not prove that product conditions survived the edit. Identify the affected output when a problem appears instead of marking unrelated assets invalid.

In a hypothetical failure, a source video explains handwashing, but the edit places it beside dishwasher footage. That could communicate a different instruction. Restore a consistent presentation or stop the use rather than adding an invisible note to a folder. The publisher needs an identifiable approved version and unresolved conditions. This prevents a later colleague from selecting a visually similar alternative that has never been checked against the original contribution, even when both files happen to share the same product name.

## Use an evidence log without treating blanks as approval

The bilingual CSV templates below include an asset identifier, original location, contribution relationship, proposed use, permission evidence, version, review state, expiry and owner. They are working logs, not permission forms, and contain no real customer information. Fill blanks from actual evidence; a default state is not agreement.

Suppose five hypothetical contributions arrive. Two have confirmed origin and use, one awaits a rights-holder reply and two have no decided channel. This describes intake states, not an industry acceptance rate. Do not report five ready assets or invent missing conditions to complete a dashboard.

Give each pending issue a next action: request the original link, establish who made the image or ask about advertising use. Clear ownership allows independent, ready assets to proceed while specific questions remain open elsewhere. Everything need not sit under one ambiguous review status. The log is useful when it tells the next colleague what is missing and what decision can follow, rather than merely preserving a count of files received. A reason for not using an asset belongs in the record too, preventing repeated requests for the same unsuitable material. Date the decision so an old reply is not mistaken for confirmation of a later use.

## Separate asset readiness from content performance

First review whether intake reduced handoff mistakes, then examine results after publication. Received, traceable, appropriate for use, accepted output and published are separate states. Completed asset counts describe production, not customer trust, sales or brand impact.

For comparisons between explanations, the [UGC testing plan](/blog/creator-ugc-testing-plan/#answer) helps define variables and observation windows. Do not attribute one post’s high viewing to its UGC label while ignoring paid distribution, audience and product differences. Keep spontaneous contributions distinct from commissioned production instead of merging them into an undefined authenticity score.

Finish with a practical improvement such as narrowing the contribution question, explaining uses earlier or improving version handoff. QuickKOL can help research creators and contact routes for a separate commission. Customer contribution origin, permissions and content decisions still need appropriate review. A good intake process makes a particular contribution usable and traceable for an identified purpose. It does not make every file universally available simply because a team has received it.

## UGC intake states and next actions

| State | What is known | Next action |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Received | A file or source reference exists | Check origin and relationship |

| Use undecided | Origin is traceable | Define channel and version |

| Conditions pending | An actual request exists | Resolve necessary evidence |

| Ready for this use | Relevant checks completed | Preserve limits in the handoff |

Editorial workflow states. Ready applies only to the confirmed use.

## UGC contribution intake log

Record evidence; the templates do not replace permission or assessment of applicable requirements.

- [ ] Separate spontaneous, incentivized and commissioned contributions.

- [ ] Keep the origin, contributor and version traceable.

- [ ] Preserve experience conditions and the proposed extract.

- [ ] Connect intended uses to an appropriate basis.

- [ ] Review cropped, captioned and translated versions.

- [ ] Assign an owner and next question to unknowns.
