# Bluesky App: Set Up a Daily Editorial Workflow

Run a first week in the Bluesky app, from a verified entry point through reading feeds, replying and handing off questions.

Start from Bluesky’s official website and follow its link to bsky.app or an app store. In the first week, build a reading routine, try custom feeds related to your actual audience, verify the source of any starter pack and reply where the team can add something useful. Assign ownership for service and official announcements. Record the original post, response time and next action. Keep what you observed distinct from a claim about the platform’s entire audience.

## Start the trial from the official app entry

The [Bluesky official overview](https://bsky.social/about) links to bsky.app and the mobile app downloads. Put the posting account, owner, official entry and recovery contact on the internal handoff sheet. Confirm the public profile link through the organization’s own website or another established route. Once signed in, check the visible bio, links and publishing time zone. Give reading and publishing separate entries in the activity log. For an agency-operated account, define the handoff and offboarding before the first shift. An editor’s ability to open the app does not authorize them to speak for the brand. Give editors and approvers a clear working route and keep credentials out of the handoff. A useful launch note also identifies which questions will be answered in public and which team owns the official help channel. Suppose the handle looks correct but the link in the profile leads to an expired campaign page. Fix the destination through the account owner before asking viewers to use it. Record when it changed so later replies point to the current page.

## Build a reading routine you can finish every day

Start with three concrete questions, such as how a term is used, which issue customers raise and whether a product update is being understood. Give each question a short reading window. Save posts that support a next action instead of transcribing the feed. Alongside accounts you already follow, try a custom feed that fits the topic. The [Bluesky custom feeds guide](https://bsky.social/about/blog/7-27-2023-custom-feeds) explains user choice among different feeds. Read the feed description and check the maintainer, topic, language and recent matches. Narrow or replace it if the sample keeps missing your question. Record that you did not see an item in the named feed and period when that is all you observed. On a second shift, compare what two editors captured using the same written question. One may read a high-volume timeline and the other a specialist feed. The different samples can be useful if they are labeled. Agree which one the team will monitor routinely and which one it will open for specific questions. Keep reading time proportional to the decisions it supports.

## Use starter packs to discover accounts and feeds

The [Bluesky starter pack guide](https://bsky.social/about/blog/06-26-2024-starter-packs) describes a bundle of recommended accounts and custom feeds. When someone shares a pack, review its curator, intended audience, the recent activity of included accounts and any stated criteria. Membership does not establish a commercial relationship. A brand can start from the pack and check relevance account by account. For a home appliance desk, a broad founder pack may lead to useful people without surfacing questions about cleaning, repair or storage. Save a few relevant sources and revisit them with the same criteria. If the team maintains a pack, explain its editorial purpose, inclusion criteria and removal process. Leave an owner and a review date so a pack can evolve when accounts change hands or their focus shifts. Suppose the brand finds a specialist account through a pack and wants to contact its owner later. Record the original profile and published contact route rather than using the curator as proof of identity or authorization. The pack helped discovery. The profile and the account holder supply the evidence for any subsequent contact.

## Match each public reply to a task

Give public replies a defined job. Answer a confirmed fact, correct your own information, direct someone to public help or acknowledge an issue with a realistic next update. If the cause is uncertain, state which part is being checked and follow up on the timing you can support. Read the surrounding thread before replying so a service response does not interrupt an unrelated personal conversation. Keep order numbers and addresses out of public replies. Use the [social media operating plan](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/social-media-marketing-operating-plan/#answer) to assign response scope, working hours and escalation. If someone asks when a product is available in one market, answer from the confirmed country and version, then route questions about other markets to their owner. A second editor should be able to find the original context and any approved wording. A brief note can say the team corrected the link at a specific time and will check the remaining language version tomorrow. Include the public link when the next shift needs to continue a conversation. Avoid capturing more personal detail than is needed to understand the issue.

## Test publishing and corrections with one post

Choose a modest first post whose facts are already approved, such as a new cleaning guide or a service policy clarification. Have another person open the destination, check the language, date and core detail, and review any image description. After publication, save the post URL, time and approved version. If someone reports a broken link, verify what the reader sees and use the official account to make the correction traceable. Keep one current route clear so several copies of a wrong destination do not keep circulating. Record any temporary support route and when it returns to normal. Use an editor with the needed authorization for changes. Suppose a support page changes while the post is being drafted. Compare the visible page with the facts in the draft and update the draft before the post becomes the reference other people share. If the team needs to pause a scheduled announcement, notify the owner who can reconcile the publishing queue. Keep the corrected statement with the decision record so a later editor can see why it changed.

## Hand off service questions with context and an owner

An editorial log can use the original link, a short description of the question, the public reply, owner, deadline and status. Separate copy corrections, service cases and product facts needing confirmation. If five replies in one thread raise a similar question, group them as one internal item and describe the observed scope. They do not estimate the needs of everyone who saw the post. For a FAQ update, use the [campaign measurement and logging guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) to label the observation window and sample. Give the issue to the person who can resolve it in the approved system. Suppose the team sees a thread about replacement parts. The social editor can explain the published policy. The service owner checks individual eligibility privately. A product owner can clarify why documentation names two part numbers. Keep those actions on separate lines so a short public answer does not appear to resolve an individual case. After the issue closes, link to the corrected public guide if it will help other readers.

## Decide which parts of the routine to continue

At the end of the week, separate reading time, resolved questions, published posts, waiting cases and operating cost. Ask whether the team found issues faster or made a public help route clearer. If the reading stream is noisy, adjust the sample. If the questions are relevant but no owner can answer them, repair the handoff before increasing publication. A small set of questions might lead to two useful corrections. Treat those as observed outcomes and keep reach claims separate. State whether an observation came from a manual log or an account reporting surface. Plan one measurable change for the second week. A desk might replace a broad feed with a local language feed and check whether it finds more usable product questions in the same time. The aim is to leave the next shift a manageable process with reliable links, a short watch list and named owners. If posting has little value but reading does, retain the reading shift and review publishing later.

## State what your reading sample covers

Record the feed, start and end time, and language used in the reading shift. If a post traveled beyond the sample, keep its available origin and follow-up links. At review, keep staff effort, public activity and account reporting in separate rows. The next editor can compare two weeks of observations without mistaking a shift change for a change in audience interest. If the team rotates languages, assign each reader a clearly labeled window and avoid combining the resulting counts as if every conversation had the same chance of appearing in both feeds.

## Hand off unresolved items at shift change

Give the next shift the approved wording, the team still working on the issue and the latest time an update is due. If someone replies to older information, compare the original post with the current help page before describing which parts still apply. One handoff card can preserve the reader’s question, next action and update time. Once a change is confirmed, check the affected post and destination so the public instructions agree. If the question belongs to a regional office, identify the market and current policy before forwarding it. Add only the information the owner needs to act. When a handoff fails, look for the missing owner or missing evidence before changing the entire publishing plan. That makes the first-week review useful even if the team posts very little during the trial.
