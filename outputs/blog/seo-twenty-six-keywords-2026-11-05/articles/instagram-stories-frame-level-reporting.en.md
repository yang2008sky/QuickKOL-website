# Instagram Stories: Report Results Frame by Frame

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Align publication time, observation age and reach definition for each Instagram Stories frame before revising the sequence.

![Three Story frame reports sit side by side with distinct observation windows.](../public/assets/blog/instagram-stories-frame-level-reporting.jpg)

Give each Instagram Stories frame its own row with publication time, intended audience, visibility duration, capture time and the metrics actually available. Compare aligned ages and definitions. Last-frame reach divided by first-frame reach is a ratio of two aggregates. It cannot tell you how many people reached by the first frame also saw the last. Keep replies, sticker actions and website sessions separate and reconcile each to its source.

## Separate the frame from the sequence

Label each frame by the job it performs, such as question, demonstration, limitation or link. Record the actual order, identifier, publication time, account and any audience selection. If another frame was inserted later, give it a new row and record when it entered the sequence. Use the [creator campaign measurement handbook](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) to label the unit and source for each number. The row for frame three should lead a reviewer to the actual asset. A rename or edit must not transfer the old version’s results to a new asset. Suppose the brand adds a price frame after the demonstration has been live for several hours. Record a different start time for the price card and keep the reason for adding it. If the same design was published in two languages, match each result to the actual language version and audience. Put the sequence as planned and the sequence as published in separate columns when they differ. That gives the creative team a reference for changing the next sequence.

## Align the observation to actual visibility

The June 2026 [Meta Instagram Plus announcement](https://about.fb.com/ja/news/2026/06/introducing-instagram-plus/) describes standard 24-hour visibility and an optional 48-hour setting in the named subscription offering. Check the account and per-frame setting actually used. Public visibility and how long reporting remains available are separate questions. Plan the early check and the closing check before launch, then record the actual capture time. If the first frame has been public for 22 hours and the last for three, their aggregate reach can mislead as a retention indicator. If one uses a broad audience and the other a restricted list, document the difference. Leave a missed capture blank with its reason. Suppose a partner shares the sequence at a later time. That share can affect the observations even if the initial schedule was identical. Record the time and known route, then decide whether to compare the sequence with another example under similar conditions. Give the export or capture its own timestamp and retain the posting time separately. A report can calculate age from those two times and note any other surface where the content remained available.

## Record which fields and definitions you actually have

The [Meta Instagram API documentation](https://www.postman.com/meta/instagram/documentation/6yqw8pt/instagram-api) distinguishes professional account access and account and media scopes. Consult the definition on the reporting surface actually used. Log only the fields the authorized report exposes, which might include reach, views, replies, navigation or sticker actions. For each one, record whether it counts unique accounts or events, the reporting time and the content covered. Distinguish blank, unavailable and confirmed zero. If the only report is a sequence or account aggregate, keep it at that level. If an outside tool uses a different name, find the definition before combining records. Suppose the account owner sends a screenshot with two similar labels and one number appears larger. Identify the exact screen and read its metric information before calculating a rate. Report a missing definition as an open item. A person may see more than one frame, so summing frame-level reach does not provide unique people across the sequence. If a different report offers deduplicated sequence reach, label its scope and keep it separate from the sum of frame observations.

## Read the frame ratio in a hypothetical example

Imagine frames A, B and C show reach of 800, 620 and 480 at the same post age. C divided by A is 60 percent. It means C’s aggregate reach was six tenths of A’s. These aggregates cannot show how many accounts saw both. The ratios between B and A or C and B also cannot identify how many people left because of a particular frame. Label the measure last-frame reach divided by first-frame reach. If C has 24 link actions, that is five link actions per 100 accounts reached on C, assuming the fields use aligned scope and timing. Action events remain distinct from unique people. Put the numerator and denominator next to the reported ratio so a reviewer can inspect it. If frame C reached a different audience, discuss the numbers independently even if the arithmetic is possible. Where the initial frame has no measurable reach, leave the ratio undefined. The figures in this example explain the method and establish no expectation for another campaign.

## Separate platform actions from destination evidence

Record which frame used which destination, language, link version and tagging convention. A platform link action, website session, form and order are measured by different systems. Use an [engagement rate metric contract](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/engagement-rate-formula-metric-contract/#answer) to document the window, count and reproducible formula. Suppose the Story reports 24 link actions while the site shows 17 sessions. Check tags, page loading, time zone and consent scope before giving the difference a cause. Keep the established facts and unresolved possibilities separate. When the same destination appears in a profile or another campaign, retain distinct source tags where available. Test the actual link in the published frame as well as the link in the brief. A revision could change the URL while leaving the label on the artwork unchanged. A product page visit can support a decision about link clarity when the evidence is in scope. Purchase attribution needs additional data and an agreed method. Send an issue about a broken destination to the page owner and retain the time the repair was verified.

## Identify what would explain a difference

The [Meta content ranking explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/06/how-ai-ranks-content-on-facebook-and-instagram/) treats Stories, Feed and Reels as distinct surfaces. For a Stories comparison, record changes in audience, schedule, content and outside activity. If frame B has lower reach, check whether it went live later, had a restricted audience or was distributed through a different route. Check the asset itself for a legibility problem. When the available evidence consists of two counts, record the observation and identify how to test a possible explanation. In a later sequence, keep the subject, order and observation window comparable and shorten just the explanation text. Look for changes in relevant questions, reach ratio and actions on the frame. A strong creative choice should have a reason the editor can verify in the actual frame. Suppose someone attributes a change to a new headline while the publishing record shows the frame had fewer hours in view. Align the age first, then see whether the evidence still favors testing the headline. Document why the next change is the one the team can actually control.

## Give the editor a concrete change

Finish the review with an observation, open question, change and owner. If the link frame reached 600 unique accounts according to the field definition, use that unit. If viewers ask where to find instructions, place the link immediately after the demonstration in the next version and preserve the same tag convention. Record the change date and both versions. If the objective is understanding a use limitation, review questions on that point separately from total reach. A confirmed production issue, such as a sticker covering the relevant detail, can justify a correction even without a metric. Explain that decision in the log. Before the next sequence, identify who will check the link, capture the frame-level report and review responses. This avoids a later search for evidence that nobody was assigned to collect. A team can also choose to hold the creative stable and improve measurement if the first run left critical definitions unclear. Write the actual next decision in terms that a new reviewer could verify.

## Retain the evidence needed for review

Share authorized aggregate data and cleared asset versions. Redact unrelated details when a report contains individual information and follow project access rules. Retain market, language and capture definitions. Before handing the file over, check that each observation points to an actual row or inspected frame. Keep illustrative calculations clearly marked, especially if the same worksheet holds live campaign data. If the reporting surface changes for the next launch, record the new definition before reusing the previous ratio.

## Put repeated captures on one timeline

If you capture a frame twice, record each observation time, remaining visibility and any intervening frame or link change. Compare two snapshots of the same frame when the field definition stays stable, and leave room to note data corrections. For cross-frame comparisons, first align hours since publication. A six-hour observation on each frame is easier to interpret than a pair taken at the same wall-clock time after staggered publishing. Suppose frame B went live late in the evening and most of those six hours fell overnight. The ages match, while the audience’s opportunity to see them may still differ. Record the time of day and consider it when selecting a follow-up. If the team adds a product explanation later, give the initial sequence and the amended sequence separate labels. That gives the reviewer a way to identify which content was visible at each observation. Also record whether the last capture occurred before or after the configured visibility window. Capture timing matters even if all final rows are reviewed together on one day. A decision to change the creative should preserve these differences in the record so the follow-up does not treat a timing change as a creative result.

## Stories frame verification checklist

Agree on collection before posting and reconcile frame by frame.

- [ ] Save published frames and their posting times.
- [ ] Confirm visibility and audience for each frame.
- [ ] Log each metric’s unit and source.
- [ ] Separate platform actions and destination results.
- [ ] Choose one change to verify in the next run.
