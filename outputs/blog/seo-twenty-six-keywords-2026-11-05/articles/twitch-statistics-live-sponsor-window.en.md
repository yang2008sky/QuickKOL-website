# Twitch Statistics: Read the Sponsored Stream Window

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Distinguish average concurrent viewers, unique viewers and the actual sponsor window in Twitch reporting.

![A streamer checks a clock and production notes beside live equipment.](../public/assets/blog/twitch-statistics-live-sponsor-window.jpg)

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

For a Twitch sponsorship review, record the actual start, end and sponsored segment. Stream-wide average concurrent viewers describe how many were watching at once on average. Unique viewers describe deduplicated viewing at the stated scope. Neither supplies sponsor-segment reach by itself. If authorized segment data is available, name its window and method. Otherwise leave segment reach open and use replay, production notes and link data to substantiate the separate facts they can show.

## Create separate rows for the broadcast and sponsor segment

Start with the observed channel, stream, start, end and time zone. Then identify when the sponsor segment actually began and ended. Use the [creator live campaign runbook](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-live-campaign-runbook/#answer) to scope the opening, disclosure, read, demo and next action. If the schedule says 8:15 but the host starts five minutes late, retain both and use the actual event for reporting. If the sponsor appears in two separated segments, give each one its own interval and version. Record the relationship between wall clock and replay time because the replay may have an offset. A break, replay cut or reconnect can move the point where a review begins. Tell the next person which record to use and whether they can reproduce it. Suppose the retailer requested both a spoken read and a visible product shot. Mark each one in the log rather than relying on a planned slot label. The log helps establish delivery without requiring an inference from audience totals.

## Use average concurrent and unique viewers for different questions

The [Twitch channel analytics guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/channel-analytics) distinguishes average concurrent viewers from unique viewers for a selected date range. A channel summary and a stream summary can refer to different sets of activity. The [Twitch stream summary guide](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/stream-summary?language=en_US) describes the post-stream snapshot. Check whether the row represents one live broadcast, several streams or a larger interval, and verify what other views are included in the actual field. Avoid assigning a channel’s monthly unique viewers to one sponsorship. Summing two stream-level unique counts also cannot deduplicate someone who saw both. Suppose a sponsor asks whether the brand was seen by every viewer in a session. An average describes a level over time. A unique count describes the population that entered the measured scope. The sponsor appeared for only part of that scope. Ask whether the account owner can provide a suitably defined segment measure, and use the broad figures as context while that question remains open. Report the unit and time window with each number.

## Calculate a hypothetical weighted average

Suppose one hypothetical stream averages 100 concurrent viewers over its first 20 minutes and 250 over the next 40 minutes. The duration-weighted average is 200: multiply each average by its minutes, add the products and divide by 60. Taking the simple mean of the two segment averages gives 175, which answers a different calculation. If the sponsor appears during just five minutes of the later period, the average of 250 does not establish that smaller window’s average. If samples are irregular, find out which duration each sample represents or use the platform’s reported field. A few screenshots do not reproduce a full segment average. Viewer minutes and unique viewers also require different labels. Under the hypothetical assumptions, 20 times 100 plus 40 times 250 represents 12,000 viewer-minutes across the broadcast. That product cannot tell you whether the same viewers stayed. If there was a disconnect, determine whether the break is included in the relevant time basis before combining reports. State that choice in the calculation note.

## Verify the sponsored action and disclosure in the replay

[Twitch branded content guidance](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/branded-content-policy?language=en_US) describes the platform’s disclosure tool and branded content requirements. Review the actual stream or authorized replay for the disclosure, product view, spoken read and next step. Keep the timecodes. [FTC influencer disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) explains clear disclosure of material connections where U.S. rules apply. Use the requirements for the actual market and placement. If a replay is unavailable, preserve an authorized recording, approved script and contemporaneous production log, and describe their limits. A settings capture can show what was configured without proving every viewer saw the disclosure. Suppose the host adds a sponsor segment after an unexpected break. Check whether the relationship was still clear when the new segment began. If someone is reviewing an edited replay, compare it to the original log and identify any missing section. Keep the evidence linked to the intended deliverable, and resolve a missing disclosure or product claim through the authorized producer before deciding whether a corrected asset is needed.

## Separate link activity from audience statistics

Record the actual link, tag, destination, time zone and campaign ID. Note whether the same route also appeared in the profile or another channel. Use the [creator campaign measurement guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) to distinguish chat link actions, site sessions, code uses and settled orders. Suppose a report shows sixty link actions and forty-five sessions. Different counting rules and measurement conditions could affect that difference. It does not establish fifteen failed visits or invalid events. If the link was posted twice, retain both times. If tracking has no segment attribution, report the correct stream or campaign scope. Check that the public path led to the intended market during the active window. A code might be repeated after the live session, so its use may be connected to the promotion without establishing a live viewing time. Agree which report the buyer will use and when it closes. Keep the result in an appropriate aggregate rather than sharing viewer details with people who do not need them.

## Read three kinds of evidence in a hypothetical recap

Imagine a two-hour event with 230 average concurrent viewers and 1,100 unique viewers in its stream report. The sponsor appears at 00:36 through 00:42. The recap can report the full-session metrics and point to the replay where the sponsor was visible. Without a deduplicated segment measure, it cannot say that all 1,100 saw the sponsor. If a site report has tagged sessions, describe attribution and time separately. Base payment review on the agreed deliverable evidence, such as the read and clear disclosure. If payment depends on specific exposure, identify the source, method and reviewer in advance. A brand can accept the production while marking an audience measure unavailable. If the next brief needs better segment reporting, plan how to collect it through authorized access. Another option is to define an acceptance criterion the producer can verify directly, such as a named placement and demonstration. Keep the hypothetical figures out of any live sponsor deck.

## Document exceptions and plan the next broadcast

Document actual duration, any restart, reporting windows, asset versions and open questions. If replay and live records differ, state which parts can be verified and who can provide more evidence. Choose one change for the next event, such as a clearer destination cue or a sponsor slot that can be logged at the required reporting scope. Recheck using the same definitions. For comparisons across streams, inspect dates, time of day, show format, promotion and host changes first. In public results, label the audience scope. Suppose the same sponsor returns for a second stream with a different guest. The producer can reproduce the segment placement, while guest promotion remains a separate change. Keep both in the log. If the sponsor’s actual question is whether viewers understood a product feature, collect relevant questions as well as viewing statistics. Give the brand an owner and date for each unresolved measurement. The next reviewer should be able to reconcile delivery, audience context and attributed actions without interpreting a single number as all three.

## Reconcile repeated reads on one timeline

If the sponsor is mentioned at the start and again midstream, give each read an ID with replay and live time. Leave distinct viewers across both intervals open unless the report provides that scope. For one total, ask which intervals it covers and how the platform or analyst deduplicated them. Verify the actual read for each segment before reviewing audience results. If an offer expired between them, identify the language the host used each time and check the landing page as well. These records make it possible to close a production issue without inventing a segment count.

## Check whether sampling can answer the sponsor question

Suppose the analyst has a snapshot of concurrent viewers every ten minutes, and the product demo lasts three. If no snapshot falls inside the demo, averaging the preceding and following readings cannot show that the segment was measured. One snapshot inside it would still be a point observation. Ask whether authorized data at the required time scope can be obtained. If it cannot, verify delivery through the replay, provide the full stream report as context and label sponsor-segment viewing as unavailable. Before the next agreement, set the minimum segment length, reporting needs and collection frequency. If the stream reconnects as a separate session, record the two reports separately and explain how any totals were combined. A brand may still need to know whether its commercial read ran twice after the restart. Mark the actual timestamps and offer conditions. If a later viewer opens the replay and uses the code, that is a separate context from a live exposure. Preserve what you know about the source without deducing a viewer’s path from the order alone. The sponsor can use the correct evidence for delivery and direct the remaining measurement question to the next production pilot.

Start with actual timing.

- [ ] Check concurrent and unique viewer scope.
- [ ] Verify disclosure and demo by timestamp.
- [ ] Keep link and order attribution separate.
- [ ] Keep one testable change for the next stream.
