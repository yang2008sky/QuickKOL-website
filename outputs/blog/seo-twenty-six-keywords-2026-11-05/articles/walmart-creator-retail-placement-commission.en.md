# Walmart Creator: Separate Placement and Commission

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Use the U.S. Walmart Creator terms to distinguish offsite links, creator storefronts and Walmart-controlled onsite content.

Walmart Creator is a U.S. creator affiliate program. Distinguish an approved link on the creator’s own channel, a curated storefront on Walmart and eligible content Walmart chooses to display onsite. Each calls for different checks on rights, attribution and settlement. Use the current official terms to confirm enrollment and commissions. Keep production and licensing fees from a brand agreement separate. A visible item link, a click and an order are each different from a settled commission.

## Check the U.S. enrollment terms first

The [Walmart Creator program terms](https://creator.walmart.com/terms), updated September 2026, list age of majority in the applicant’s jurisdiction, U.S. residence, an active Walmart.com or SamsClub.com account and a restriction on current employees of either company. The application connects at least one publicly viewable social account and Walmart evaluates acceptance. Record the market, named program and actual approval status before a booking. A storefront screenshot alone cannot confirm a new applicant’s enrollment. The combined terms cover more than one retail program. Name the retail destination, exact product and responsible party in the brief. Have the creator handle identity and payment setup through the official flow. The brand’s project folder can usually store approval status, dates and a public destination without retaining tax or bank documents. If the team finds older instructions that look broader, check the current agreement and official onboarding for the account in question. Keep written evidence of the decision relevant to the actual market and placement.

## Check the link and item for an offsite post

Section 2 of the [Walmart Creator program terms](https://creator.walmart.com/terms) defines qualifying links and related restrictions. Obtain the matching link from the program or approved platform and check that the destination is the qualifying item shown in the content. Open the destination on a phone and compare SKU, size, model, color and market before publication. If an item is unavailable, confirm a substitute through the program or collaboration owner and update the copy and footage together. A normal product URL can help with research. The commission question depends on a valid qualifying link and program tracking. Save the approved link, published placement, version and review date in the handoff. Check any restrictions or approvals before adding paid distribution. If a buyer reaches a similar product instead, do not assume it counts the same way. A link check should record what the viewer actually sees and whether the call to action still describes that destination. Assign one owner to monitor changes during the active placement.

## Use the storefront for a defined assortment

The [Walmart Creator setup guide](https://creator.walmart.com/education/new-to-walmart-creator) describes creating collections and publishing a storefront, with desktop publication distinguished from mobile editing in the current guide. Check the actual public view before sending traffic. If a kitchen demonstration features a single model, decide whether the viewer should land on that product or a collection. Name collections clearly and assign someone to maintain them. When an item goes out of stock, avoid describing a similar substitute as the one in the shot. Record the review date and destination rather than promising the retailer’s pricing and inventory will remain unchanged. Suppose a seasonal collection includes the items shown and several alternatives. Label them so readers can distinguish the demonstrated product from other options. Test the storefront from the public perspective and preserve the asset version used with it. If the creator keeps a collection after a campaign ends, agree who updates links and whether old promotional copy can still appear. Storefront publishing is a different operational action from placing a qualifying link in an outside post.

## Evaluate Walmart-controlled onsite use separately

Section 27 of the [Walmart Creator program terms](https://creator.walmart.com/terms) gives Walmart discretion over whether and how to display eligible content onsite. Onsite commissions apply to direct qualifying purchases as described in the section and payout terms, with a dedicated Storefront ID required for eligibility. Submission leaves placement uncertain. Distinguish an offsite post, delivery of an onsite candidate and actual onsite use in the assignment. Use a dated placement observation and the relevant program report to support claims about display or credited purchases. Review Sections 10 and 27 for rights and compare the program scope with the separate brand license. A deliverable can be approved by the brand while the retailer has yet to use it. If the fee depends on retailer display, identify who verifies it, when and with which evidence. Suppose two products share similar packaging. Match the placement and report to the exact product identifier before attributing a sale. A creator’s ability to upload a file leaves the subsequent publishing decision with the retailer.

## Put disclosures where viewers see the endorsement

The [Walmart creator disclosure guidelines](https://creator.walmart.com/influencerguidelines) require a clear explanation of the connection, including visual and audible disclosure in video and placement requirements for hashtags. [FTC influencer disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) explains why viewers should understand a material connection, such as a commission, gift or paid assignment. Review the actual shot, audio, caption and destination together. A profile note may be missed by someone who watches only the video. Have a reviewer watch with and without sound and check whether the relationship is clear next to the endorsement. Where a brand separately pays for production, describe the actual relationship accurately. Product experience claims should reflect what the creator used and observed. Suppose a shopping list includes an item that was provided free and others the creator bought. Identify the relevant connection and apply the program’s disclosure rules for the placement. Save the cleared wording with the clip version. If the team later crops or recuts the video, check that the disclosure still appears long enough and near enough to the recommendation to be understood.

## Track clicks, qualifying purchases and settlements separately

Sections 5 through 7 of the [Walmart Creator program terms](https://creator.walmart.com/terms) and the applicable payout terms govern qualifying activity, attribution and payment. Actual credited transactions depend on the relevant window, order conditions, tracking and exclusions. Returns or refunds can affect commissions. The [Walmart Creator setup guide](https://creator.walmart.com/education/new-to-walmart-creator) describes Impact account, verification and payout setup. Suppose an early report shows twelve tracked orders and two later returns. Use the actual eligibility and settlement statuses rather than assuming ten commissions must be paid. Record market, item, link, attribution window, status, currency and commission source. Resolve definition issues before comparing results. If the client asks for a forecast, separate illustrative arithmetic from an expectation and rely on actual account terms for any rate. A marketing report can show recorded clicks and pending orders as early observations. A finance report can reconcile credited amounts after the settlement period. The two reports may run on different schedules. Give their readers dates and an owner for adjustments instead of silently replacing an earlier total.

## Keep brand fees and affiliate commissions in separate records

Use [a brand deal memo](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/brand-deals-commercial-memo-content-brief/#answer) to specify production, offsite publishing, candidate submission, review, revisions and fixed fees.

Use the [creator usage rights guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-rates-usage-rights/#answer) to define version, territory, duration, advertising and the person who can grant permission. For a performance payment, align the brand contract with aggregate data the program can supply and identify eligible statuses and the close date. Suppose the brand wants to use a creator’s onsite clip in its own advertising. Check program terms, asset permission and retailer restrictions before planning that distribution. Paying for production does not itself give the buyer a retail placement or sublicensing rights. The commercial handoff can track what the creator delivered, what the brand accepted, what Walmart used and what the program credited. Put an owner and evidence link on each row. If something remains conditional, leave it open until the evidence arrives. That allows accounts payable to process the agreed work independently from future commission revisions and lets the next campaign plan from actual placement information.

## Verify the customer path on release day

Open the public path on a phone and check the disclosure, the featured model and the actual landing page together. If availability differs by market, record where the check was performed. Assign an owner for stock changes, incorrect links and program updates. After a correction, save the revised link and time alongside the originally released version. This keeps a later report interpretable if the campaign used different destinations before and after the fix. If the team cannot confirm the current item in the target market, avoid attributing activity to it until the placement is reconciled.

## Assign the owner for later edits and removal

Suppose the creator learns that a product package changed while the old video remains visible. Record the observed location, original SKU and approved edit, then ask the retailer or brand owner whether the link, creative or supporting copy needs a revision. The offsite publisher can manage the post on its own account. Walmart controls its onsite placements, so route an onsite issue through the current program process. Separate a creative correction from a commission adjustment so the older activity is not silently assigned to the newer cut. At closeout, inspect the agreement and program terms for continuing rights and assign any archive or removal request to the responsible owner. The team should also review an inventory change on the actual destination before replacing a product link. If the new destination describes a different use case, give the replacement its own approved script and evidence. Maintain a version record that names the post, storefront or onsite location, who can act on it and which date the customer-facing change was verified. This gives marketing and finance a way to tell which campaign conditions applied when a tracked event occurred.

## Walmart Creator placement records

| Placement | Evidence | Separate record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offsite post | Actual qualifying link for the item | Disclosure and tracking scope |
| Creator storefront | Public collection and version | Maintenance and market |
| Walmart onsite content | Observed placement and program record | Onsite eligibility and settlement |

Use the current agreement and actual reports for commission and eligibility.

## Walmart Creator handoff checklist

Verify against the actual placement and account report.

- [ ] Confirm program, market and enrollment.
- [ ] Match each link to the exact featured item.
- [ ] Verify the disclosure viewers see.
- [ ] Separate brand fees from commission.
- [ ] Mark unresolved placement, payment and clearance.
