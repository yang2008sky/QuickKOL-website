# Instagram Ads: Preflight Creator Assets and Placements

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Review objectives, placements, assets, creator permissions, landing pages and measurement before launching Instagram ads.

![Two production staff review vertical ad storyboards and a phone preview.](../public/assets/blog/instagram-ads-creator-placement-preflight.jpg)

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

Before launching Instagram ads, define the business goal and measurable action. In Ads Manager, check eligible Instagram placements, creative crops, landing pages and reporting. For a partnership ad using a creator identity or asset, verify the exact accounts, creative version, permission, territory and term. Treat organic approval, disclosure, asset rights and ad account access as separate handoff items.

## Choose an objective around an observable action

Start with what the viewer should do after the ad: recognize the product, read instructions, submit a form or buy. Have the media owner verify whether the objectives and optimization available in the account support that action. Use the [creator campaign measurement guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-campaign-measurement/#answer) to agree on the primary event, supporting events and observation window. Suppose the brand wants to test a new installation page. Decide whether the result is a page visit or a completed installation. A click or arrival cannot establish completion. If visits are the only reliable data, scope a narrower traffic test. Record the objective, budget, market and condition for stopping. A team may run a short test to validate a destination before testing a lower-funnel event. Mark the test purpose so later reviewers do not compare it directly with a sales campaign. Assign the owner who can confirm event delivery and the person who will act on the result. This keeps the launch from depending on a metric that the available setup cannot report.

## Verify the ad identity and permission

The [Meta Instagram partnership ads announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/creator-marketplace-for-brands-and-creators-to-collaborate-on-instagram/) explains how advertisers can amplify eligible organic content or create a new ad using a creator or partner’s handle. Use the [partnership ad permissions guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/what-is-whitelisting-creator-ad-permissions/#answer) to record the brand and creator accounts, exact asset, permission route and expiration. Match the platform permission to the agreement. Organic publication approval establishes the scope of that post. The ability to select an ad still leaves asset and audio clearance to confirm. Keep credentials and usable access codes out of the project handoff. Have the relevant account owner handle identity changes. Suppose a brand has permission for one Reel from a creator and prepares a new cut with a different title and music. Review whether the agreement and current platform permission cover the actual new version. A clear record can show that the creator approved the edit, the correct ad account can run it and the publisher is authorized to use that identity. Give each item an owner and end date, then review renewal before the campaign runs beyond the term.

## Review each eligible Instagram placement and crop

Check which Instagram placements are eligible for the objective, account and asset. Feed, Stories and Reels can present the same material differently. Save the approved preview for each planned placement and inspect subject, captions, brand and next action. Watch with sound and muted on an appropriate screen. Suppose a crop hides the hand and connection point in a product demonstration. Choose a placement-specific cut or adjust the planned delivery with the media owner. Inspect the actual preview rather than relying on the source master. Keep placement, file name, approver and review date. A safe-looking frame in one placement may become hard to read when another shows more interface elements. Give the caption and disclosure enough space for the smallest intended display. If you cannot reproduce a placement preview, mark it pending and confirm the actual supported setup before relying on it. A short placement matrix can tell the next editor which creative version was approved and which element required changes.

## Test the actual destination on a phone

Check the button, language, region, offer end date, product model and page behavior. Have a tester follow the path as a first-time visitor and locate the intended action. Record the URL, tracking parameters, version and test date. Where a site event is unavailable, state that limit. Do not claim results from orders the setup cannot substantiate. If the in-app browser and a standard browser behave differently, record both observations and route the problem to the owner. If price or stock changes, review the actual ad wording against the current page. Give the team a way to verify the correction before launch. Suppose a lead form opens but uses the wrong country’s privacy notice or contact workflow. Resolve that destination issue independently of how the creative looks. A successful click test confirms the link at the tested moment. It cannot establish the experience for all markets. Ask a reviewer in scope to check the language and availability that will be advertised.

## Review rights, claims and disclosure in the actual ad

Review the cleared asset, caption, ad identity, paid scope and audio together. [FTC influencer disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) emphasizes making material connections clear where U.S. rules apply. Check the actual ad preview for applicable disclosure and platform labeling, as well as the original draft. If a 30-second creator review becomes an eight-second ad, check whether a usage limitation disappeared or an endorsement changed meaning. Review language, rights and applicable local requirements for a new market. Archive the approval for each version separately. Suppose a clip was licensed only for one month of paid use. A revised crop may still be within scope, but a longer campaign or new territory needs its own check. Record who can approve an extension and when to stop delivery if it is unavailable. Before launch, confirm that all reviewers inspected the same file and caption. If an editor updates a claim or soundtrack after approval, reopen the relevant review rather than relying on the old version’s clearance.

## Give ad delivery its own reporting scope

Keep the campaign, ad set or ad identifier, attribution window, currency, delivery scope and extraction time. Impressions, reach, clicks, site sessions and orders use different counting rules. The [Meta ads transparency explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/02/increasing-our-ads-transparency/) provides context for public ad information. Public visibility cannot substitute for an authorized delivery report. Where the asset also has an organic post, keep organic performance separate unless the reporting surface documents a combined measure. If clicks are high and sessions are lower, inspect page behavior, tags and metric definitions before deciding whether to revise the destination or the ad. Confirm who can see the needed aggregate. A change in attribution window can affect which actions appear in the report even with the same creative. Record the setup before comparing periods. If the campaign started in one market and then expanded, preserve both the earlier scope and the change date. The next buyer can check which observations are comparable instead of treating a single cumulative result as a complete explanation.

## Assign launch, pause and renewal ownership

Use one launch record for asset, account, market, destination, budget, timing, permission and measurement. Name who can publish, pause or revoke access. If a creator authorization ends Friday but delivery is planned for the weekend, arrange a stop or renewal before launch. After activation, open the preview and destination and confirm the actual version. For an issue, log ad ID, evidence, action and verification time. At closeout, separate platform results from the brand’s acceptance of the deliverable. Suppose a pause is needed because the wrong landing page is attached. Confirm which ad is affected and who can stop it. Verify the new link in the revised preview and record when the corrected version went live. That keeps reports for the two placements from merging incorrectly. The next owner should be able to read what ran, under which permissions, with which measurement scope and what still needs confirmation.

## Keep the next test tied to one change

If the available data shows clicks but no confirmed completion, first verify the destination and event. If those work, choose one creative change such as the opening or instructions. Record audience, time, placement and metric definitions before repeating. If both creative and distribution have to change, report a comparison of two campaign setups and keep the causal question open.

## Rehearse a pause and restart before spending

Imagine a creator licenses a demonstration for one market. The brand plans Stories and Reels delivery. In preflight, the Stories crop hides a disclosure and the Reels button points to an older model. Hold activation while the editor repairs the Stories frame, the web owner confirms a current destination and the creator reviews any change that affects the approval. Close the two issues separately. A placement that still has an open issue cannot inherit the other placement’s approval. Attach the signoff to the exact asset and ad identifier. Now suppose the monitoring owner finds the older ad is running. Verify the actual delivery and permissions, then have the authorized media owner pause the affected ad. Record the former version, restart time, budget effect if known and the report windows. Describe the observed impact without using clicks to estimate how many distinct people were affected. If the new destination is in a different market, review regional availability and language before resuming. The report for the relaunch should have a start date and enough version history to explain why there are two rows. This exercise can expose whether the team actually knows who can halt spending and what evidence is required to resume. A written pause decision should include a condition for restarting, an owner and an update time. When permission has expired, make renewal part of that condition even if the new edit and link are ready. This connects the production, rights and media checks to one version the whole team can verify.

## Instagram ads launch checklist

Confirm each item with its owner before launch.

- [ ] Define business action and available reporting.
- [ ] Inspect each planned placement preview.
- [ ] Verify creator and asset permissions.
- [ ] Test destination and tracking scope.
- [ ] Assign pause, renewal and closeout owners.
