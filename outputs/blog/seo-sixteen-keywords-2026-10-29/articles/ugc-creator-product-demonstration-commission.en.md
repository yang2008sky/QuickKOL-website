# UGC Creator: Commission a Product Demonstration That Works

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Separate product use, filming requirements, permissions and acceptance so a UGC creator commission produces an asset your brand can actually use.

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

A UGC creator commission usually buys creator-produced product content. Publication on the creator’s account, advertising use and later edits are separate decisions. Define one use problem, arrange genuine product experience, and agree on facts, shots, files, permissions and acceptance. A conversational presentation should come from understanding the product, rather than pretending that a paid commission is an unsolicited customer opinion.

## Separate the asset from the audience

In this context, UGC describes creator-produced material with a lived-in, user-oriented presentation. It does not establish that a video is an unsolicited customer review. Buying a file and buying publication on a creator’s account are different assignments. One requires a useful asset and appropriate permissions; the other also requires a distribution position, publishing arrangements and audience evidence. Combining them under one vague deliverable makes both pricing and evaluation difficult.

Use the [creator scope checklist](/blog/creator-contract-scope-checklist/#answer) to separate production, creator publication, advertising use and source-file delivery. When distribution is not included, follower count may be less useful than a clear demonstration, convincing explanation and the ability to capture a product detail reliably. When publication is included, attractive production alone cannot establish that the account reaches the intended people.

There is also a difference between conversational delivery and presenting a paid participant as an independent customer. The [FTC endorsement FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) addresses commercial endorsements and brand relationships in the US context. The purchasing distinctions here are editorial recommendations; a casual filming style is not a disclosure exemption. Establish the destination before evaluating candidates, because that choice changes the brief, quote and review process.

## Turn the product question into a filmable assignment

Avoid a request for an authentic video that converts. State the uncertainty the viewer should resolve: how a lid comes apart for cleaning, how software hands over a task, or whether a bag fits a particular storage space. A single primary question produces a more coherent demonstration than ten disconnected selling points. For a technical or B2B offering, the useful next action may be a demonstration request rather than an immediate purchase.

Specify the intended viewer, existing question, required product action, verifiable facts, prohibited implications and one next step. Showing a removable component is different from claiming that the product never leaks. The latter needs evidence from the brand; the creator should not be expected to fill that gap with confident delivery. The [executable campaign brief guide](/blog/write-a-useful-campaign-brief/#answer) provides a fuller structure that can be reduced to the needs of this production assignment.

Write an acceptance sentence: the viewer can see opening, removal and reinstallation without captions obscuring the connection. That is easier to evaluate than a request to make the product feel premium. Assign responsibility for the setting, lighting and props. Resolve special devices, adapters and account access before starting the delivery clock.

## Make product experience part of the schedule

Provide the correct model, instructions and a functioning sample, then distinguish delivery from usable product experience. A shipping confirmation does not establish that the creator has tested anything. If installation, compatibility or repeated use matters, start the relevant production period after those conditions exist. When the intended experience cannot be completed, change the assignment to an unboxing, feature demonstration or attributed brand explanation instead of retaining a fictional long-term testimonial.

[FTC disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) addresses truthful experience and material brand relationships in the United States. Our operational recommendation is to invite questions about confusing instructions, missing parts and unexpected behavior before settling the final scene. Such feedback may reveal a useful issue in the product or receiving experience rather than a production failure.

Watch statements whose meaning can change in an edit. Completing an installation today does not support a claim that anyone can finish it within minutes. Retain relevant conditions and avoid unsupported health, financial or performance implications. If experience differs from the proposed story, agree on an objective demonstration or end the assignment under the agreed payment terms. A creator should not need to convert an unfavorable experience into praise to be paid for completed work.

## Evaluate the work that the assignment actually needs

Watch complete examples rather than the strongest three seconds of a reel. Can a viewer follow the action? Is the audio usable? Do captions obscure the feature? Does the explanation leave enough time at the difficult step? Discuss the same assignment with candidates, rather than ranking unrelated public view counts or asking several people to deliver a complete usable advertisement for free.

A small paid test can resolve a specific production uncertainty. Define the segment, fee, permitted uses and whether advertising is included. Testing how to show a reflective connector is a bounded task; requesting an entire marketing concept under a test label is not. If a work sample and production discussion already answer the question, another filming exercise may add little.

Separate necessary conditions from preferences. Fast editing may be unsuitable for an assembly sequence that needs patient explanation. Accent, setting and clothing decisions should relate to the intended audience and assignment, rather than unrelated personal assumptions. Ask how the creator would explain the difficult step and what resources they would need. That provides better evidence than a demand to copy every movement in someone else’s video. Retain a few assignment-specific selection reasons for the later review.

## Define the permission before the file arrives

File delivery, ownership and permission to use the content are separate questions. The [U.S. Copyright Office overview](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) explains original expression and authorization by rights holders; the actual agreement still needs to fit the relevant jurisdiction and project. Payment, source-file delivery and the label UGC do not automatically mean perpetual worldwide use, unrestricted editing or permission to transfer the work to others.

Confirm the user, channels, territories, term, editing, translations, advertising and renewals separately. Can the brand turn a demonstration into a different testimonial? Are source footage and music included? May a retail partner use the result? Advertising through the creator’s account also has platform conditions; downloading a file does not establish permission to advertise through that account.

Keep a permission record the media buyer and editor can use: asset identifier, allowed use, start and end dates, approving contact, restrictions and evidence location. Resolve both permissions and technical requirements before scheduling production. Otherwise, a well-made asset may arrive without the rights the campaign needs. Treat an expanded request as a new discussion instead of asking an editor to interpret a general instruction that the client needs it.

## Create review points that prevent avoidable reshoots

Confirm the outline, essential action and factual statements before filming; inspect difficult shots before a finished edit; then review content and technical files at delivery. Assign one person to consolidate comments so the creator does not receive incompatible requests from the brand, agency, product team and sales. Every project does not need shot-by-shot approval. Put review points where a mistake would actually be expensive to reverse.

Distinguish factual errors, missing agreed work, stylistic preferences and new scope. An incorrect model name is different from a late decision to change the setting. Agree on revision rounds, feedback format, response windows and reshoot conditions. Record delays caused by late samples, product changes or faulty equipment rather than assigning every missed date to the creator.

Check aspect ratio, resolution, caption placement, sound, the closing action and filenames. List clean versions, separate captions or silent files in advance when they are needed; none should be assumed to be universal inclusions. Ask someone unfamiliar with the product to describe the steps after watching. That can reveal an explanatory gap, but it is a small comprehension check, not a user study or proof of sales performance.

## Compare complete usable-asset costs

Consider a hypothetical procurement example, not a market rate. Offer A has a $600 production fee, $200 for a limited permission scope and $100 in shipping and production extras, totaling $900. Offer B costs $750 including the same permission scope, with $100 in extras, totaling $850. Those totals are comparable only if the work, deliverables, term and treatment of taxes are equivalent.

If A includes two genuinely different videos and B includes one, choosing solely on total cost would miss the difference. Six export sizes from one edit are not six independent creative concepts. Compare filming, concept count, edit variants, publication and permissions as separate units. Identify excluded costs such as urgent delivery, additional reshoots and later renewals.

Consider whether use conditions have already been resolved. A technically acceptable file that still needs music or advertising permissions can create scheduling costs. For repeat procurement, record timely delivery, reasons for revisions and the proportion of accepted usable assets. Avoid turning a single subjective score into a permanent judgment about a creator. The cost sheet should explain a purchasing tradeoff, not imply a guarantee about future views or conversions.

## Finish the handoff before calling the asset ready

Assign a stable asset identifier and connect the approved version, permissions, captions and next user. Dropping files into a chat does not tell a media buyer which version is cleared for which destination. Before public use, confirm that the product version is current, the link reaches the right page and necessary disclosures survive cropping and caption overlays.

Use explicit states such as awaiting review, accepted, correction required, new scope or paused. Acceptance means the agreed assignment has been met; it does not establish commercial returns. Any advertising test needs its own objective, window and comparison method. Performance under different audiences, bids and destination pages cannot all be attributed to the person who produced the video. Translate feedback into a concrete production question, such as which step remains difficult to understand.

Before commissioning another asset, identify the unresolved question. The next useful deliverable might be one close-up or a different use setting rather than a repetition of the entire demonstration. QuickKOL can help structure the brief, research relevant creators and prepare personalized contact. The parties still need to confirm product experience, permissions and delivery. A clear acceptance state gives the next commission a reliable starting point.

## Four components of the commission

| Component | Confirm | Do not assume |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Production | Demonstration, files, formats and acceptance | Creator publication is included |

| Publication | Account, placement, timing and disclosure | Every follower sees the post |

| Reuse | Channels, term, territories and edits | Unlimited advertising permission |

| Source footage | Files, music and permitted editing | Files transfer copyright |

Editorial procurement framework, not a universal contract or a market rate.

## Before sending the commission

Keep a record both parties can inspect.

- [ ] Express the product question as a filmable action.

- [ ] Confirm usable samples and genuine experience.

- [ ] Separate production, posting, permissions and footage.

- [ ] Assign one feedback owner and define revisions.

- [ ] Hand over cleared assets with the permission record.
