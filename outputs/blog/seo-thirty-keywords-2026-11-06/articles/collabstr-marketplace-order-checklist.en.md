Collabstr offers preset packages and custom offers. Before ordering, specify whether you need a creator to post, deliver an asset to the brand or also license it for a defined ad use. Verify the version, review clock, revision conditions and payment status in the actual order. Give the reviewer the deadline before delivery and confirm any automatic completion rule that applies.

## Write what you are buying in one sentence

An office supplies brand may need a forty-five-second desk organization video delivered as a file that it will publish on its own account. Another brand may want a creator to publish once on their account and retain a public link. Those orders have different deliverables, review steps and evidence. Use the [campaign brief guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/write-a-useful-campaign-brief/#answer) to identify purpose, publishing account, audience, final file, captions, deadline and acceptance criteria. Examine two work samples that resemble the assignment and check whether the creator can produce the required action, environment and language. A polished room tour may be poor evidence for a tight product demonstration in which the viewer needs to see a small latch close. Save a specific timecode that shows why the creator fits. Ask what equipment, location or product lead time the work depends on. If the assignment requires someone to speak on camera, include a short example and the required language. Give each candidate the same requirements so quoted packages can be compared on a consistent scope.

## Choose between a preset package and a custom offer

The [Collabstr buyer order guide](https://help.collabstr.com/en/article/how-to-place-an-order-with-an-influencer-j0fgkl/) describes purchasing a preset package from a profile or sending a custom offer with a platform, content type and price. A preset may suit a standard assignment. If you require raw footage, multiple openings, brand publication, ad rights or another account, check whether those items are actually included. A package called one Reel may leave publication, duration or tagging unclear. Ask before ordering. If the answer changes the scope, put it in the offer and accepted order rather than relying on a vague chat message. Consider two quotes. One includes an edited file with captions and a round of revisions. The other includes a public post and a clean file, with captions left to the buyer. Compare what each would cost once the missing work and permissions are included. If the deliverable needs shipping, identify who provides the item and when the creative clock starts. Keep the accepted offer and current terms with the order record. A marketplace listing can change after you save it.

## Give the file, publication and reporting their own criteria

Write a checkable specification for each deliverable. A vertical edit might need captions, one visible product action and a recognizable version name. A publishing assignment also needs the account, copy, link, public window and any retention period. If the buyer wants campaign reporting, specify the metric, window and authorized evidence. Organic viewing is not an assured delivery outcome. A production file can pass its review before the buyer’s publishing checks. A creator post calls for someone to verify the real link and the agreed copy. Separate corrections of brief defects from new creative requests. If the promised video has no visible demonstration, ask for the missing action with a timecode. If the team later wants a second audience hook, discuss a scope change. Review the file at phone size so the specified action, disclosure and captions remain legible. When the creator and brand publish related edits, label each asset and its account separately. Assign a report owner before launch so a platform screenshot does not become the only retained evidence.

## Define permission by use, channel, term and territory

The [Collabstr buyer order guide](https://help.collabstr.com/en/article/how-to-place-an-order-with-an-influencer-j0fgkl/) notes that usage rights are not always included. Use the [creator rates and usage rights guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-rates-usage-rights/#answer) to distinguish organic brand publication, reposting, ads, editing, territory, term and raw footage. Thirty days of organic use on an owned account does not establish permission for a paid ad on another platform. Review music, fonts, other materials and people shown in the clip against the same intended use. If the order has a rights field, include the limits that matter and review the scope both parties accepted. If the brand wants to crop or add an opening card later, determine whether the accepted permission permits that edit. If a creator offers a clean file without audio, identify who supplies cleared music for the advertising version. Store evidence of acceptance and the relevant asset version together. When rights expire, assign the removal or renewal decision to a person who can see where the asset is used. That avoids approving a campaign asset that someone cannot later locate.

## Put the review clock in a named person’s hands

The [Collabstr creator order guide](https://help.collabstr.com/en/article/managing-orders-x16v8g/) describes a seventy-two-hour buyer review window after submission, automatic completion for no response and an associated payout buffer. The buyer guide describes the ordering, review and approval flow. Check the current instructions and deadline in the actual order, record the time zone and assign a primary and backup reviewer. Coordinate any schedule change before an expected delivery falls on a holiday. If something is missing, request a specific revision where the order allows it and verify the resulting status. Do not assume that sending a message in another channel changes the platform deadline. Have someone check the order after submitting feedback to confirm it reached the correct state. Suppose the buyer’s reviewer works in California and the creator submits from the U.K. on a Friday. Translate the order deadline into the reviewer’s working hours and make sure the backup can open the correct file. If a second submission starts another clock under the actual order rules, record the new date and what changed.

## Reconcile funding, refund destination, approval and settlement

The [Collabstr buyer order guide](https://help.collabstr.com/en/article/how-to-place-an-order-with-an-influencer-j0fgkl/) says payment is captured on order and a declined order returns funds to the account balance. Review the real records for completion and release status. An account balance credit is separate from a refund reaching a bank card. For a cancellation or dispute, retain the order ID, relevant status, time and amount, then use the current support process. Separate platform charges, production, rights, shipping and total in the finance record. Mark receipt only from the evidence the finance owner can verify. In a hypothetical order, a 220-dollar edit and a 100-dollar rights add-on produce 320 dollars before any separate charges. If the creator declines a revised offer, confirm which amount was held and which destination received a credit. An operations team may know that the order closed before finance can match the transaction. Preserve both statuses. A corrected order should reference the earlier one so another person does not count both as delivered work or authorize a duplicate payment.

## Test the decision on a specific delivery gap

Suppose edit A shows the product but captions cover the critical step. Edit B fixes the captions and uses a track with unresolved rights. Ask for the corrected captions with cleared audio or the clean version allowed by the original brief. If the review clock is close, verify the revision state inside the order. Track the file review and ad permission separately while that usage remains open. An approval click cannot supply a missing commercial license. If raw files were outside scope, treat a request for them as a new item. Use the same exercise for a public post. A correct video on a different account may fail the agreed publishing deliverable even if the production file is excellent. The buyer can identify which item passed, which needs a revision and what would close it. Where there are multiple reviewers, collect their comments in one approved set. Competing requests can delay delivery and make it difficult to distinguish a required correction from a preference.

## Hand off the approved version and next action

Before closing, give operations the actual file, account, release date and rights expiry. Give finance the amount, charge details and observed status. Give the next reviewer the brief, revision evidence and approved version. If ads are outside the agreed use, mark the rights question for a separate decision. Before repeating an order, review which fields were missing from the earlier delivery. Reconfirm any change of account, medium or region. If one package has become a recurring collaboration, agree on a stable naming and approval process without assuming that later orders inherit every earlier term. Leave a record of the time the buyer approved and the items that still require follow-up. A new campaign manager should be able to find the correct edition and tell whether it can be reused. For an issue still open with the marketplace, record the support reference without duplicating private payment information in the shared campaign folder. Imagine that a second colleague saved an unapproved edit. Check the shared folder and publishing record, then use the order evidence to identify the current approved version. Treat a proposed new crop or soundtrack as its own edit request with a rights and schedule check. If platform support is still reviewing a case, the follow-up owner needs the current status and next review date. Save an authorized copy of the order facts your organization relies on. A marketplace status can later change, and the campaign folder should still explain when the company accepted the work, who reviewed the permitted use and what the finance team could verify. If a deadline was extended, include the accepted time and reason in the handoff.

## Collabstr order acceptance

| Item | Evidence | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production | File, captions and version | Does it meet shot and format requirements? |
| Publication | Account, copy, link and date | Was it published in the agreed place? |
| Rights and payment | Scope, deadlines and status | Who approves and reconciles? |

Use the terms and timer displayed for the actual order.

## Order and acceptance checklist

Mark each item in the actual order record.

- [ ] Package and custom requirements aligned
- [ ] Production, posting and rights defined separately
- [ ] Review deadline and backup named
- [ ] Payment status and final files traceable
