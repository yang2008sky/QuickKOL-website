# SocialCat: Review a Creator Collaboration Offer

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Review collaboration type, delivery, rights, payment and closeout before accepting a Social Cat offer.

![An editorial graphic separates a creator offer into sample, deliverable and payment records.](../public/assets/blog/socialcat-creator-offer-handoff.jpg)

QuickKOL editorial graphic; not actual account metrics or a platform interface.

Before accepting a Socialcat offer, verify whether it is gifting, paid work, affiliate commission or a combination. Compare the proposed work and permissions with the compensation. Confirm shipping, deadlines, approval, ad use, payer and follow-up. Receiving a gift, accepting an offer, permitting a use and receiving payment are distinct states.

## Verify the offer and actual brand first

The [Social Cat invitations guide](https://help.thesocialcat.com/en/articles/1680066) describes receiving an invitation and moving into a matched or active workflow after acceptance. Open the actual offer and verify brand, assignment, contact, expiry and collaboration type. If email and platform messages differ, confirm the request through the original record or a verifiable brand route. A direct message alone does not establish payment. Ask specific questions while details are unclear. An urgent reply request does not resolve missing terms. Keep the message date and confirmed contact without collecting unrelated identity information. Suppose a brand profile names a retail product but the person contacting you refers to a different company. Check whether they are an authorized representative and which entity will supply the sample or pay for work. A legitimate company can work through an agency. You still need to know who makes and can approve the actual offer. Avoid opening an unknown attachment to prove the connection. The goal is a reliable project record that both parties can recognize later.

## Separate gifting, a production fee and affiliate commission

The [Social Cat collaboration types guide](https://help.thesocialcat.com/en/articles/3296066) distinguishes gifting, paid and affiliate work and describes direct brand-to-creator handling of paid or affiliate compensation. Ask whether a product is gifted, loaned or expected back, and who bears applicable shipping charges. A fixed fee needs amount, currency, payment trigger and date. Commission needs eligible orders, returns, reporting, review date and payer. If an offer has all three, keep separate lines. Use the [creator rates and usage rights guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/creator-rates-usage-rights/#answer) to account for scripting, production, revisions, posting and permissions. Suppose a product has a stated retail value of 60 dollars and the request includes two videos plus revisions. Retail value is not available cash for your expenses. Estimate the time and costs you would actually incur. If the brand offers a share of future sales, leave the payout uncertain unless supported orders exist. Ask how you can verify the results that affect you. A clear offer lets both sides distinguish a sample that arrived from compensation that remains due.

## Check whether the work fits your production schedule

Put delivery, testing, scripting, filming, revisions, approval, publication and evidence into a schedule. Suppose the brand wants a final edit the day before a launch while shipping still takes two days. Ask whether the publication window can change. Agree how to pause, replace a sample or end the job if the item cannot be used. Use the [campaign brief guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/write-a-useful-campaign-brief/#answer) to confirm product facts and demonstrable actions early. If the model or instructions are missing, confirm what you can while leaving the deadline conditional on arrival. For a brief that requires a comparison, ask what comparison is supported and what can be shown safely. A sample photo before shipping can help identify the version but does not establish that you received it. Write who will confirm arrival and when creative review starts. Give yourself time to check whether the product you received matches the brief. If the merchant requests a later launch change, identify which existing commitments are affected before agreeing to an additional edit.

## Separate file delivery, organic publication and advertising permission

If the offer says one video, ask whether it means file delivery, posting on your account or both. Specify duration, ratio, raw footage, captions, revisions, account and publication date. State which channels the brand can use, for how long and whether editing, paid media or sublicensing is included. A sample’s arrival is not evidence of the agreed usage scope. If the brand later asks to turn an organic post into an ad, confirm the new use, music and material permissions and any revised terms. Record the approved version and intended destination. A request for a caption correction can be within scope while a second hook for another market may be a new deliverable. If you need to grant authorization through the platform, verify the actual account and scope first. Do not send a password as part of publication or rights approval. Ask who removes or renews the asset at expiry. The creator should be able to find which file was cleared for which use.

## State the actual commercial connection and product limits

For an endorsement with a material connection in the U.S., the [FTC influencer disclosure guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) covers clear disclosure of gifts, payment and other connections. Review wording, placement, platform tools and applicable rules for the actual content and market. Receiving a product for free can still matter to the disclosure. Keep claims within what you can accurately demonstrate. If the brief asks you to repeat a statement that lacks support, ask to use different wording. Retain the final copy and approval version. Suppose a maker asks you to call a cup leakproof. If the assigned film only shows it upright, the footage cannot establish that claim. Ask for support or describe the demonstrated action accurately. The posting account should review the disclosure where someone will actually see it, including in the video if relevant. An editor can provide a clean version for a separate authorized use, while the published version carries its required information. Give the brand the approved wording before upload so a late edit does not remove context.

## Compare two hypothetical offers before accepting

Suppose offer A provides a sample for one video and thirty days of organic reposting. Offer B provides the same sample and 180 dollars for two edits, one revision and three months of paid use. Compare production effort, permission, posting obligations and real expenses, not only the stated product value and fee. Ask for the territory if B omits it. If A requires you to cover shipping, recalculate actual costs. If an offer pays commission only, verify eligible orders and reporting before relying on any earnings. This example illustrates scope and is not a market rate. A creator may be willing to make one simple demonstration for a product they will use, yet require compensation for an ad and additional edits. If the requested schedule leaves too little time for a proper test, propose a narrower scope or a different date. Write any accepted change into the actual offer or confirmation rather than assuming a quote automatically updates the platform record.

## Set review dates for delivery, revisions and payment

On delivery, record version, link, time and permitted use. Compare revision requests with the agreed brief. Review fixed payment and commission under separate records. For a posting assignment, retain the actual post and disclosure. For file delivery, verify the version received by the brand. Keep receipt pending when evidence is missing. Use the permitted aggregate agreed by both sides for order tracking and avoid asking for customer data. Identify who handles late payment and when to review it. If the brand says an invoice was processed, distinguish its internal status from money you actually received. A report that shows attributed orders may not yet reflect refunds. Update the relevant row when the final report becomes available. If terms change, save the accepted revision and which delivery it affects. An unresolved commission question should not obscure a production fee that was already paid or a file that still needs approval.

## Close the work with a clear record of ongoing permissions

Give the brand the approved version, publication evidence and expiry. Keep your own payment and raw footage status. If a loaned product must be returned, retain shipment and receipt evidence. Confirm renewal before another use starts. Apply the same fields to the next offer and verify actual terms again. Record any unresolved issue and which question would have surfaced it earlier. An orderly close gives you a way to respond if a buyer later refers to an expired asset or a different quote. It also helps you estimate future workload from what happened rather than from how simple the original message looked.

## Reconfirm an offer that changes from gifting to required posting

Suppose the original note offers a sample for evaluation. After it arrives, the brand asks you to promise a post that week and grant advertising rights. Compare the original scope with the new request. Confirm whether publication is required, the deadline, deliverables, payment and permission. You can propose a revised fee and date or complete the commitments already agreed. If sample terms remain unclear, resolve whether the product should stay or be returned. Put the accepted outcome in the project record. If the brand wants to quote earlier feedback, confirm the excerpt, media and term as well. Keep the gift, ad authorization and actual payment in their own states. A buyer may want a launch date before it has finalized the brief. Give a realistic conditional date and specify when the production clock starts. If you cannot meet the timing after a late sample, let the contact decide whether to reschedule or narrow the assignment. Save a short message that confirms the final deliverable and commercial scope. A later reviewer can then see exactly where the project changed from sample evaluation into a publishing commitment. If the parties did not agree to that change, record the open decision and avoid sending a file labeled approved for use by the brand.

## Social Cat creator offer review

| Area | Verify | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample | Delivery, costs, gift or loan | Terms and receipt |
| Asset and rights | Production, posting, revisions and ads | Accepted scope and final version |
| Payment | Fee, commission, dates and payer | Report or verified receipt |
