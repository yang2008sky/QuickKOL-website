# Bluesky Social Media: Plan Brand Moderation

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Plan replies, filtering, platform reports and shift handoffs for a brand Bluesky account.

![A print studio owner holds a tablet and a blue paper sample at a workbench.](../public/assets/blog/bluesky-social-media-moderation-brief.jpg)

AI-generated editorial illustration, not an actual creator, client, product or event.

Before a brand uses Bluesky for public communication, define which questions it answers, who speaks for the brand and when to involve support or safety staff. Separate brand replies and visibility controls, additional labeling services and platform enforcement. Check the tools available in the actual account, keep necessary incident evidence and give feedback, potential policy violations and urgent issues separate owners.

## Set a response promise your team can keep

Suppose a small print studio uses Bluesky to answer questions about paper, colors and shipping. The account owner checks the queue twice on local working days. Publish a service promise the team can meet and direct individual orders to the authorized support route. Use the [social media operating plan](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/social-media-marketing-operating-plan/#answer) to assign publishing, response, order handling and safety escalation. Put hours and a current contact route in the profile. If holidays are uncovered, publish the relevant schedule and plan major announcements when someone can respond. Offer local routes where policies differ by market. A response promise can distinguish when staff normally check and when a case owner can provide an update. That helps when a question reaches the channel outside working hours. Write down who covers a sick day and who can approve a correction to a public post. If the answer depends on information held by the shipping team, assign a way to ask them and a time to follow up. A profile can stay useful even when its social account cannot provide individual service.

## Classify the request before choosing a response

Separate product questions, feedback, individual support, repetitive harassment, possible rule violations and urgent risks. If someone reports a late delivery, acknowledge the feedback and provide the official support route without requesting order numbers, home addresses or phone numbers in public. Route verifiable product criticism to the relevant product or editorial owner. A recurring question may deserve a public answer that can be shared again. Privacy or safety concerns need a different escalation owner. Use the [Bluesky public conversation guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/bluesky-social-brand-public-conversation/#answer) to keep the relevant context. A disappointed customer, a question asked in good faith and an abusive message call for different decisions even if all three appear under a launch post. Staff can document a short reason for the response class without labeling the person. Agree on how to handle sarcasm or unclear language. When the message is ambiguous, a private internal review can be more appropriate than either a public accusation or an automatic removal. If the same complaint reveals a real product issue, track the resolution separately from any platform action.

## Separate feeds, filters and platform enforcement

The [Bluesky Terms of Service](https://bsky.social/about/support/tos) distinguish Discover recommendations from third-party feeds. What one brand account sees depends on its feed and settings and does not represent every user’s view. The [AT Protocol moderation documentation](https://atproto.com/guides/moderation) describes labels and subscriptions. The [Bluesky Community Guidelines](https://bsky.social/about/support/community-guidelines) distinguish baseline platform rules from additional labeling services. Record which mute, block, reply and reporting controls are available in the actual account. For each control, note who can use it, which interactions it affects and how it can be reversed. A local visibility result does not establish that content disappeared from the service. Ask two authorized colleagues to review a harmless test post in their normal clients and compare the agreed evidence. If their feed settings differ, document the difference instead of inferring a platform-wide result. Consider whether a moderation setting is appropriate for the brand’s goal, such as reducing repeated interruptions to support staff. Keep a record of the setting version so the next person knows which configuration was in effect during a pilot. Review implications before adopting a third-party labeling service.

## Document a potential policy report concretely

Describe the observed content and the applicable rule when preparing a platform report. Record the relevant link, account, time, language and scope. For private information or threats, preserve only authorized evidence and avoid amplifying the material publicly. The shift worker can record the handoff while the appropriate owner decides whether to submit. The [Bluesky moderation inbox announcement](https://bsky.social/about/blog/10-30-2026-moderation-inbox) on October 30, 2026 introduced a beta with updates on reports, account notices and appeals. Check whether the actual account has access. Even with a report status, decide whether the underlying issue needs a separate internal response. A platform review may not address an order problem, staffing concern or security question. Keep the two workflows distinct in the case record. If someone asks for a public statement before the facts are clear, an authorized owner can acknowledge receipt and explain when the next update is expected. Keep unsupported assumptions out of a report. Use the minimum relevant context to explain why it may fall under the rule you selected.

## Assign separate owners for replies, corrections and escalation

Routine product questions can be answered from approved materials. If a price, promotion term or product statement is wrong, have the designated owner approve a public correction. An unexpected account access issue goes to the authorized security owner with the discovery time and actions already taken. Use the organization’s safety process for urgent issues. Staff should know which account and materials they can use when replying. Specify when the brand can add a clarification to a thread and when it needs a separate statement, including who retains the version history. Every route needs a receiver and a follow-up point. Suppose a scheduled post shows an outdated shipping date. The reviewer can pause the scheduled duplicates, verify the correct date with operations and put the update where readers of the original can find it. If a reply addresses an individual order, it should not promise a resolution that staff have not confirmed. A short decision note can record what was known and who approved the wording at the time.

## Rehearse a shift with three synthetic messages

Start with a question about paper weight and have the shift worker answer from public specifications. Then use a damaged shipment message. Move it to the approved support route and verify the receiving owner. For a third exercise, use synthetic content to rehearse handling the posting of someone’s private contact details. Practice limiting exposure and preparing an escalation without submitting a real report. Give the next worker only the shift record and see if they can identify the next action. Add status and owner fields where responsibility is unclear. Use an offline exercise or appropriate test environment if a rehearsal could affect a live account. It may help to include a regional handoff where one team is ending its day and another is starting. Check whether both know which records they can see and who can update the public thread. A dry run that discovers a missing support route is a valid pilot result. Fix the routing before announcing the channel to customers.

## Review how issues were handled

After a two-week pilot, review routine response time, unresolved support cases, recurring product questions and platform report status. Examine these as separate categories. The number of reports alone cannot characterize account health. If public replies are prompt but order problems wait two days for assignment, improve the support handoff. If several people ask the same specifications question, check the product page and account information before writing more replies. Keep the exceptions and open cases in the review. Document whether the timing sample contains overnight or holiday cases so another person can interpret it. If response time improved after adding staff, note the staffing change. A quick answer also needs to be correct and useful. Read a small sample of closed cases for the right destination, clear wording and a responsible follow-up. Avoid using the moderation log as a general audience research database. When a question is no longer relevant to the case, leave it outside the retained summary.

## Leave a readable handoff and exit rule

At each shift change, record the account scope, unresolved cases, next review time, responsible owner and approved language. If staffing cannot continue, update the service promise and official support destination, then assign any open cases. Avoid asking users to provide the same information again after handoff. Decide how long to check the account after the pause and who reviews access and settings. A brand that runs a new pilot later can reuse the parts that worked, such as routing an order question, while updating tools and staffing assumptions. Keep the moderation plan dated so people can distinguish a prior experiment from the current commitment.

## Test the boundary with two similar public replies

Imagine two replies questioning the color of a new print. The first shows a photo without personal information and points out wording on the product page. The second repeatedly publishes someone else’s contact details. For the first, ask the product or editorial team to compare the actual specifications and correct ambiguous wording if warranted. For the second, route to the safety owner, preserve necessary evidence and avoid spreading the details while reviewing the applicable policy. The common topic does not make them the same kind of case. In the recap, check separately whether the product explanation changed and whether the risk was handed over. If the platform gives a status, record whether it concerns a post, account or other object. Ask a second reviewer to classify both messages from the training brief. If they reach different conclusions, refine the examples before asking staff to act independently. Give the public account team a short approved response while the specialist team handles details in its own system. This keeps both the customer feedback and the safety case visible to the people responsible for them.

## Bluesky response routing

| Category | Action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product question | Verify and reply publicly | Shift or product |
| Order case | Route to official support | Support |
| Possible violation | Document and report as appropriate | Moderation or safety |

Choose the response layer based on the case.

## Brand account shift checklist

Run an exercise before opening the pilot.

- [ ] Response scope and hours are clear
- [ ] Support and safety owners confirmed
- [ ] Actual visibility and reporting controls reviewed
- [ ] Shift and retention rules assigned
