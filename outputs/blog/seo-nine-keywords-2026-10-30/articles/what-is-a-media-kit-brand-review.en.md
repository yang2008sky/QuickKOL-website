# What Is a Media Kit? A Brand’s Guide to Reviewing Creator Evidence

A media kit is a package introducing a creator, their work, audience and partnership direction, presented as a webpage, document or platform-provided format. It helps decide whether to continue discussion, without automatically verifying every number or establishing accepted pricing and usage rights. Connect material information to an account, source, date and actual task, then ask focused follow-up questions.

## Use the kit to decide whether further research is worthwhile

A useful kit identifies the creator, typical work, examples relevant to the task and an appropriate contact route. It does not need to replace complete research. The early decision is whether another discussion is worthwhile, not whether the creator will disclose every private metric before receiving a concrete brief.

The [YouTube Creator Partnerships guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9385307?hl=en) describes a customizable Media Kit for eligible creators, with country and qualification limits. This illustrates platform-supported preparation without establishing the same feature for everybody or automatic verification of any platform-styled PDF.

Connect receipt date, version and project. A general introduction may remain useful while information relevant to this decision needs updating. Do not rank by visual polish alone or treat the absence of a PDF as evidence that someone cannot work professionally. A public site and relevant works can provide a valid research entry. What matters is whether the information helps judge the task. The brand’s review should therefore distinguish a missing document format from missing evidence, and request only the additional material that can change the next decision.

## Confirm the person, account and role

A name, handle, agency and account can differ. Retain public account links and stable identifiers, checking whether the displayed work belongs to the same subject. If an agent handles contact, confirm an appropriate public or authorized role. A shared name, similar photograph or forwarded screenshot alone does not establish identity.

The [creator shortlist research guide](/blog/creator-shortlist-workflow/#answer) helps connect accounts, work and contacts. Team accounts and individuals may perform different jobs. Specify who appears, produces and publishes. Team experience in a kit does not establish that every member participates in this assignment.

Collect necessary business and task information without guessing private email addresses or homes. Ask a concrete question when something differs, such as which account owns a work or who handles delivery. Preserve pending states rather than letting polished design or recognizable client logos replace identity checks. Screening should reduce misunderstanding, not produce a verdict about a person’s authenticity without evidence. The recipient should be able to distinguish an account verified through an appropriate route from a name merely copied into the sheet by another colleague.

## Read metrics through source, object and time

Followers, views, unique audiences, watch time and interactions are not interchangeable. A kit should connect a number with its platform, account, content, window and source. A historical high can illustrate work without guaranteeing future performance or representing every current publication.

[YouTube audience documentation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416?hl=en) defines monthly audience as estimated unique viewers over the previous 28 days and Top geographies by watch time. If a kit uses these fields, do not rename them total followers or subscriber-country shares. Summed video or platform totals do not automatically become different people.

Retain the source, capture date and verification state of each decision-changing number. Creator screenshots, authorized platform observations and third-party estimates are different evidence categories. Ask about a missing definition instead of calculating new numbers from precise-looking graphics. The brand needs information sufficient for this decision, not a supposed independent audit of every visible field. Keep these distinctions when summarizing for a client, since a qualified figure in the original kit can otherwise become an unqualified claim after it passes through an agency presentation or shortlist table.

## Use work examples rather than logos as capability evidence

Choose work resembling the current task and record its question, creator role, format and visible outcome. A client logo does not identify deliverables, usage conditions or commercial effect. Without public evidence, do not add generated sales for that brand or demand restricted client information.

If a case includes numbers, check original disclosure, scope and attribution. Brand campaign results, agency summaries and creator observation are different evidence. An anonymized process explanation or public work can support a bounded capability judgment when confidential material cannot be supplied.

Work should also reveal the next question. A clear demonstration may leave live-hosting capability unresolved; a relevant audience may leave genuine trial time unconfirmed. Ask about the missing condition rather than treating a complete kit as complete task fit. Capability research concerns particular work, not automatic qualification from brand prestige or partnership count. This helps a small brand assess a useful specialist with fewer recognizable logos as fairly as a larger account with an extensive presentation, while still identifying actual prerequisites for production.

## Return scope and indicative prices to the actual request

A kit may list formats and indicative rates, but without a brief it often cannot cover specific production, publication, revisions and permissions. Establish the purchase behind a price before comparison. From pricing is neither a quote for every requirement nor acceptance of a particular date.

The [U.S. Copyright Office overview](https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/) provides background on expression and rights. Receiving a kit or paying for production does not replace confirmation of actual asset uses. Advertising, translation, retail distribution or source files need explicit discussion rather than being inferred from open to collaboration.

The [creator rates and licensing guide](/blog/creator-rates-usage-rights/#answer) helps separate work and use. Ask about currency, deliverables, scope and exclusions before negotiation. A kit starts discussion; acceptance needs a version, confirmations and an appropriate process. This is a brand review method, not a ready-to-sign universal agreement or a claim that a public rate remains valid. Keeping those states separate lets the outreach team express interest without accidentally committing production or letting a later stakeholder assume that a displayed package includes everything the business subsequently requests.

## Handle stale and inconsistent information with focused questions

Check update dates before deciding which gap matters. An older biography may remain accurate while an audience chart needs newer evidence. Do not request a complete redesign to verify one field, or call different observation dates an error or deception without investigation.

State the object and purpose of the question. For example, explain that the assignment needs local viewing relevance and ask for an available shareable report retaining its label and date. Allow unavailable, inappropriate or restricted answers. Missing is not zero, and unverified does not mean incapable.

Retain the reply as another evidence item, identifying its provider, checks and unresolved matters. If an essential condition cannot be obtained, pause or choose a smaller task rather than filling the sheet with unexplained estimates. Reviewing a kit is research whose quality comes from clear questions and boundaries, not maximum file collection. Reduce unnecessary sharing to prevent later misuse. The next reviewer should be able to see which uncertainty was resolved and which remained, instead of assuming that a received document completed every verification automatically.

## Download the evidence index and make the review reproducible

The bilingual CSV downloads below provide an evidence index with three starter categories: identity, metrics and commercial scope. They are blank template rows, not actual creator information. Each record can retain an account or asset, source type, link or reference, metric object, window, receipt date, state, permission scope, owner and next question.

One claim per row is easier to reproduce. Follower scale and viewing geography belong in separate rows because their objects and sources may differ. Link public work where appropriate. For restricted evidence, keep a suitable internal reference rather than sending private material to unnecessary recipients.

Do not write verified without saying whether the check concerned identity, definition, observation date or actual platform access. Blank cells can remain pending and owners update states. The index connects questions and evidence rather than automatically scoring a creator. A reviewer still decides whether the information supports another partnership discussion. This gives the receiving team a practical way to use a polished kit, a simple webpage or several documents consistently without pretending that their formats provide the same level of verification.

## Use two evidence rows for a numerical example

Suppose a kit lists 10,000 followers and 20,000 views for some content. Use separate rows retaining platform, account, content, time and source. They cannot become 30,000 different people. These are hypothetical numbers, not an actual kit or industry standard.

If the viewing window is missing, mark that row pending and ask which period and content object it describes. An owner can research the work before deciding whether an updated report is necessary. The index supports partial progress without invented facts to make every state green. Retain a new evidence version rather than overwriting the initially incomplete record.

## Close the review with a task and next question

Suppose a B2B software brand reviews a teaching-focused kit. It can identify relevant demonstrations, retain audience-source limitations and ask whether the creator has time for genuine use of a test environment. This hypothetical situation is not a client case, and the kit’s views do not predict demo requests.

Record the outcome as further research, a specific brief, one field request or no continuation this time. In outreach, identify the work reviewed, proposed discussion and pending conditions. Do not expect the creator to infer the entire business requirement from a context-free send your kit request.

What is a media kit ultimately returns to purpose: an introduction and evidence entry for discussion. Connecting sources, dates and task makes it executable research rather than only a polished document. The next colleague should understand why to continue, what is missing and who confirms scope, instead of inheriting an unexplained attachment. A focused next question is a valid review outcome even when the team is not ready to quote, commission or activate any content use.

## Kit information and the next question

| Information | Check | Next question |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Identity and account | Subject, links and role | Who produces, appears and publishes? |

| Work | Task, format and actual role | How does it match this task? |

| Metrics | Object, source, window and date | Whom does the number describe? |

| Commercial formats | Scope, timing and pricing conditions | What must be confirmed for this use? |

For the next discussion, not automatic verification, a final quote or acceptance.
