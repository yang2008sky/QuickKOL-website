# How to Get More Instagram Followers With a Co-Post

Status: local review draft; confirm the publication date before release.

Choose a relevant creator partner and test whether a collaborative post leads to follows and continued interest.

![A creator adjusting a phone to film a demonstration for a co-post](../public/assets/blog/instagram-kol-co-post-follower-pilot.jpg)

To grow a relevant Instagram audience, run one co-post with a creator whose viewers have a reason to follow the brand after the campaign. Prepare the profile and next post to fulfill that reason. Agree on the publisher, collaborator acceptance and data owner before release. Review content reach, any available content-attributed follows, account-level net growth and later engagement separately. Use the follow-up to decide whether the collaboration reached people who want more of the brand’s work.

## Define who should follow and why they would stay

If a brand serves first-time apartment gardeners, its target might be people interested in low-maintenance growing. Write down what they would receive after following, such as one small experiment each week. Avoid substituting a temporary discount or giveaway for the ongoing content offer. Select a creator who can demonstrate the work and has original examples relevant to the audience’s language, setting and questions. The subject should fit what both the brand and the creator can plausibly keep publishing. A public follower count does not reveal which viewers have a reason to visit the brand’s profile or keep watching.

Give useful growth two stages. The first is relevant distribution and a reason to open the profile. The second is a follow followed by an opportunity to use the next piece of content. Aggregate reporting can help observe those stages. The brand does not need individual follower records. Use an [Instagram profile content pilot](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/instagram-followers-profile-content-pilot/#answer) to determine what the account can maintain after launch. Put the follow-up posts and release timing in the brief before signing a creator. Name the person who will respond when viewers bring real questions back to the account.

## Give the co-post one practical problem to solve

Give the creator a problem that is easy to demonstrate on a phone, such as watering windowsill herbs without leaving standing water. Show the method. Close with a clear statement about the next kind of example a follower can expect. Let the creator explain it in their own style. Have the brand check objective product claims, disclosure and the correct account. [Meta’s Instagram Collabs announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/08/music-and-collabs-on-instagram/) describes a shared-author format after an invitation is accepted. Test actual availability, format and publishing controls before committing. A mention in a caption is different from co-authorship. Co-authorship also does not establish permission for paid advertising.

Record who creates the post, who accepts, when to check and who can edit the caption. Confirm that both parties can see the expected publishing state. If a creator’s account is unavailable or the release moves, use an agreed fallback, such as a brand-owned version with a separate creator share. Give that version its own delivery status, because distribution may differ. Avoid posting a duplicate or changing the reporting window while the original collaborator invitation is still unresolved.

## Prepare the profile before sending new visitors

Follow the path from the creator’s post to the brand profile. Check whether the bio explains who it serves, whether the pinned work fulfills the promise and whether the next action is easy to find. If the demonstration promises a weekly balcony watering card, pin a first example and make its use case visible in the title. Align destination, offer, marketplace and product specification. Give the profile one primary action for the pilot instead of asking viewers to follow, join an email list, download and shop at the same time.

Ask someone who has not seen the campaign plan to test on a phone. Starting from the creator’s work, can they tell why they would follow and which piece to open first? Correct an accidental regional account or old campaign asset before launch. Keep the handle, pinned version and initial profile screenshot. If the team changes the profile midway through the activity, log the change and time. Otherwise the recap can confuse the effect of a better profile with the effect of the creator post. The pilot is easier to learn from when every material change has a time and owner.

## Lock the data you can obtain and the baseline

Before publication, preserve a recent comparable sample in the same format and a matched account growth window. Note ads, holidays and other creator partnerships. [Meta’s Reels metrics explainer](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/) describes watch time and information about follows from Reels. Use the fields the participating accounts actually provide. Net account change reflects gains and losses over a period. A follow metric attributed to a content item covers the scope defined by that report. Do not fill both columns with the same number or add the two together.

Attach account, asset, time zone and extraction time to each metric. When the format does not provide a field, record the gap. A brand comparing two creators should request comparable observations over the same post age. If one supplies only an ending screenshot, do not treat the other creator’s richer report as evidence of a better result. Use an [Instagram net change and follow events guide](https://www.quickkol.com/blog/instagram-followers-check-net-vs-gross-change/#answer) to verify the units and choose a main measure the pilot can reliably obtain. Compare changes only across comparable units and dates.

## Separate organic, paid and concurrent activity

Have the paid media owner record the original post, ad creative and campaign ID. If the team promotes it, preserve actual delivery dates, markets and objectives. Ask the creator to note other public activity in the same period, such as a livestream, press appearance, follow-up or new release. Run one live acceptance check for the expected account presentation, sponsorship disclosure and route to the brand. [Instagram’s creator best practices announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/) describes professional dashboard education on creation, engagement and reach. Confirm the reporting fields in the actual account.

Suppose the creator co-posts on Wednesday, the brand starts ads on Thursday and a journalist shares the post on Friday. The brand account gains followers on Saturday. Each event can be recorded. A period total does not isolate their contributions. Report that several sources overlapped and attribution remains limited. If the team needs an organic discovery test, reserve an agreed observation period before starting paid promotion. Mark any exception with actual delivery times. That also prevents paid distribution costs from disappearing when the team compares the cost of several ways to reach a new audience.

## Read an example without overstating the lift

Suppose native reporting shows 12,000 accounts reached, 150 profile visits and 42 follows attributed to the content under the available report. Over the same period the brand reports 80 gains and 24 losses, for 56 net follows. Present 42 and 56 separately. One is a content-level measure and the other is account-level. If you divide 42 by 12,000, label it reported content follows per reach under the same window. It is not a profile visit conversion rate unless the available data establishes a common funnel and population. Look at the next relevant post, without inferring from aggregate activity that a specific new follower returned.

If there are many profile visits and relatively few follows, inspect the avatar, bio, pinned examples and offer, then review audience fit. If a post drives follows and later activity weakens, check whether the brand fulfilled its content promise. Record evidence that can directly support a conclusion and turn uncertain reasons into hypotheses. Change one controllable factor in the next round, such as the opening demonstration or pinned sample. A simultaneous overhaul is harder to evaluate.

## Decide whether to run another partnership

Close the pilot with three questions: did the team deliver the contracted work, did relevant viewers show interest, and can the brand keep publishing the subject? Publication acceptance does not establish that a measurement target was met. If evidence is missing, fix the reporting or narrow the next brief. One option is to ask the same creator for a second installment, keep the period and intended audience comparable, and clarify the profile promise. If viewers mainly asked about a different product, revisit the target group before increasing posting frequency.

Make a one-page handoff. For the creator, record published scope, verifiable results and interest in another round. For the brand, retain profile changes, reporting window, paid delivery and other relevant events. Use the agreed visit, follow or engagement evidence to decide whether to expand the budget. Obtain appropriate approval before putting the creator into longer ad runs or adapting their footage. The handoff should make clear which decisions this one pilot supports and what needs verification in the next agreement.

## Use the follow-up to decide whether to renew the collaboration

Suppose a gardening brand and creator co-publish a small-space planting demo. Profile visits increase, but the brand follows up with a sitewide discount instead of the planting tips promised in the post. First accept the creator’s contracted work on its own evidence. Treat the profile and follow-up as variables the brand controls. Publish an item that actually continues the theme, then observe available engagement and visits. Aggregate data can describe the overall response and leaves individual follow histories unknown.

When several partnerships can be compared, preserve the same elapsed window, pinned profile version, paid start time and concurrent brand activity. If two rounds produce relevant questions or content-attributed follows, discuss a defined series with the creator. If interest appears concentrated around a temporary offer, the next brief should specify what the brand can continue delivering when it ends. That makes renewal a decision about audience and creative fit. If the follow-up changes topic, record the change in the recap and run one more observation on the original theme.

## Growth evidence from a co-post

| Field | Object | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | This asset | Accounts reached under that report |
| Profile visits | Visits in the selected report | Keep source and definition |
| Content follows | Follows attributed to the asset | Use platform definition |
| Net account change | Whole account in the period | Separate gains and losses; attribution limited |

## Co-post pilot checklist

Use one handoff sheet for creator and brand checks.

- [ ] Confirm audience and sustainable content promise.
- [ ] Test co-authorship, disclosure and profile route.
- [ ] Confirm reporting fields and time zone up front.
- [ ] Review content follows, net growth and later action separately.
